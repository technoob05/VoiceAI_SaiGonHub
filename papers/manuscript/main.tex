\begin{abstract}
On a $120$-clip Vietnamese tonal minimal-pair corpus ($20$ carrier syllables $\times$ $6$ tones, MMS-TTS-vie synthesis), a contrastively learned $32$-d tone embedding ($26.05$~KB on disk; same-syllable-different-tone hard negatives) coupled with a $260$-d segmental stream and gated by an AND-existence voting rule reaches \textbf{$92/100$ correct-miss on tonal variants at $(\tau_A, \tau_B) = (0.85, 0.93)$} and $95/100$ at $\tau_A=0.90$, both at $120/120$ self-lookup recall, saturating at $100/100$ at $\tau_A=0.90$ under DEMAND-like additive noise at $\mathrm{SNR}\in\{10, 5\}$~dB. This is a $+56$~pp absolute lift over the same gate driven by a heuristic 6-bin tone histogram, exposing the prior $36\%$ ceiling as a tonal-representation limit rather than a voting-rule limit. On cache-hit, end-to-end latency drops to $25$~ms, $\mathbf{1{,}441\times}$ over the $36.5$~s FP32 cold cascade on representative single-utterance runs (the ratio is short-utterance peak; for longer inputs the cache-hit pathway preserves order-of-magnitude wins). The cache short-circuits ASR, MT, and TTS entirely on hit. A parallel diacritic-preserving hash on the T2 text cache, AND-gated with a diacritic-stripped stream, reduces semantic collisions $\mathbf{95.8\times}$ (from $16.48\%$ to $\mathbf{0.17\%}$) on a $3{,}000$-transcript audit at zero paraphrase-recall cost ($20/20$). Sentence-level adversarial Vietnamese minimal pairs, which previously presented a $46.7\%$ false-hit failure mode under utterance-level tone pooling, drop to $\mathbf{16.7\%}$ under a training-free syllable-local max-pool over the same $32$-d learned tone embedding (and to $\mathbf{3.3\%}$ when the pooling head is itself contrastively trained on the adversarial pairs), confirming that the residual was a pooling-locality limit rather than a representation limit. The full INT8 cascade (PhoWhisper-small + NLLB-200-distilled-600M + multilingual VITS) fits in $861$~MB and runs CPU-only. Tone-bin structure is Vietnamese-orthography-aligned; cross-language reuse requires per-target re-binning (Mandarin: $5$, Thai: $5$, Yoruba: $3$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A translation cache that confuses \emph{ma} (ghost) with \emph{m\'a} (mother) is not one percent wrong; it is wrong in a way that no industrial user can trust. The contrast extends through the full Vietnamese tone inventory: \emph{m\d{a}} means tomb, \emph{m\~a} means horse, and \emph{m\`a} is a contrastive conjunction. Every one of these distinctions rides on a single suprasegmental contour that segmental acoustic features compress away. Two acoustic realizations that differ only in tone are, to a music-style perceptual hash \citep{wang2003shazam}, indistinguishable; to a downstream worker reading the translated output on a wrist-mounted display, they are six unrelated sentences. This is the failure mode that motivates the present work.

The deployment scenario sharpens the problem. A recent industrial speech-translation challenge targets factory floors, construction sites, and hospital wards, where workers' hands are occupied, network connectivity is intermittent or absent, and ambient noise is loud and persistent. The defining property of speech in such environments is repetition: most of what a worker says during a shift is a short directive drawn from a small canonical vocabulary, safety calls, equipment names, location markers, uttered again and again by the same speaker and re-uttered across the rest of the crew.\footnote{Field surveys we draw on report 40--90\% of utterances per shift as canonical directives. The target hardware is a Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 with a Hexagon NPU; the application envelope is a sub-1.5~s end-to-end latency budget within 4~GB of NPU memory, with no cloud round-trip permitted.} That regularity is the lever. If the same phrase has been translated once, it should not be translated again, and the cost of recognizing it the second time should be orders of magnitude lower than the first.

The existing cache primitives, however, all fail in this setting. Exact byte-level hashing of raw audio achieves a hit rate of zero on real speech, because no two utterances, not even repeats by the same speaker, are bit-identical after analogue capture, voice activity detection, and chunking. Generic perceptual audio hashes, descended from the music identification literature \citep{wang2003shazam}, achieve high robustness to additive noise but are explicitly engineered to discard the linguistic content that defines tonal contrast; on the six-way Vietnamese minimal-pair set, off-the-shelf perceptual hashes collapse all six tones into a single bucket. Neither family was designed for the cache primitive on-device translation actually requires: a fingerprint whose collision structure tracks phrase-level \emph{semantic} identity in a tonal language. The recognition layer is well understood, with PhoWhisper \citep{le2024phowhisper} providing a strong Vietnamese ASR baseline, but the caching layer above it is unaddressed.

If those claims hold, an entire class of industrial deployments, currently blocked on either cloud latency or on-device compute, becomes feasible on hardware that already fits in a worker's pocket.

\textbf{Contribution roadmap.} We close the gap with two clusters of contributions. The \emph{first cluster} is what is novel about the fingerprint itself. (i)~A 272-dimensional tonal-perceptual fingerprint built from five linguistically grounded blocks (a 6-d Vietnamese tone histogram, a 130-d temporal-subsampled MFCC trajectory, a 130-d first-difference delta, 4-d F0 statistics, and 2-d energy statistics) engineered so that two recordings of the same phrase hash together while two tonal minimal pairs hash apart. (ii)~A two-stream voting cache that treats the 6-d tone histogram and the 260-d segmental remainder as independent fingerprints and requires \emph{some} stored entry to pass both stream-specific thresholds before issuing a hit; on a Vietnamese tonal-minimal-pair corpus this achieves a $72\%$ correct-miss rate at the present $\tau_{A}=0.85$ operating point, where every single-vector concatenation we tried collapses to uniform false hits.

The \emph{second cluster} is what makes the design deployable. (iii)~An INT8 cascade substrate (faster-whisper, CTranslate2-NLLB, dynamically quantized VITS) that compresses the FP32 cascade by 5.4$\times$ on CPU and, when the cache hits, by a further three orders of magnitude (25~ms end-to-end on a representative single-utterance run, $1{,}441\times$ over the cold FP32 cascade). (iv)~A typology-aware voice activity detector that gates on F0 stability rather than energy, recalibrated to the natural Vietnamese pitch range after a generic-speech default rejected the majority of in-domain audio. (v)~A reproducible log of four abandoned alternatives (an MFCC speaker cache, coarse-to-fine MT routing, weighted-concatenation fingerprints, PCA-whitened single-vector fingerprints) that collectively rule out single-vector fixes and identify two-stream voting as the load-bearing design decision.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} surveys the on-device speech-translation, mixed-precision quantization, audio-fingerprinting, and tonal-speech literatures and locates the missing primitive precisely. Section~\ref{sec:method} formalizes the fingerprint and the two-stream voting architecture. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports the fingerprint-component ablation, the tonal-minimal-pair evaluation, end-to-end latency, precision-recall calibration, cache scalability through $N = 1024$ entries, and cross-language generalization to Mandarin, all with bootstrap confidence intervals over $\geq 1000$ resamples. Section~\ref{sec:deployment} reports the on-device memory and latency profile. Section~\ref{sec:limitations} expands the negative results. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{On-device speech translation}\label{sec:related:s2st}

Speech-to-speech translation has converged on two architectural families. Cascaded systems compose an automatic speech recognizer, a neural machine translator, and a text-to-speech synthesizer, typically instantiated with multilingual backbones such as Whisper for recognition and NLLB-200 for translation~\citep{nllb2022}. End-to-end systems collapse the cascade into a single model, with SeamlessM4T~\citep{seamlessm4t2023} the current reference point for high-resource directions.

Both families were originally designed for cloud inference, and their on-device viability rests on a downstream engineering substrate. The CTranslate2 runtime~\citep{ctranslate2} and its faster-whisper~\citep{fasterwhisper} wrapper provide INT8-quantized Transformer inference with custom CPU kernels, while GGML-style quantization~\citep{ggml2023} ports the same idea to mobile-class CPUs through aggressively packed 4-bit and 5-bit tensor layouts. These engineering tools, however, are language-agnostic: their kernels treat every layer and every language uniformly.

As a consequence, the on-device speech-translation literature has concentrated on high-resource pairs where degradation under uniform quantization is acceptable, and reports for tonal low-resource languages such as Vietnamese remain sparse. The combination that this work addresses, namely end-to-end Vietnamese translation, fully on-device, with tone-preserving quality on industrial speech, is largely absent from existing benchmarks, and no public system reports a deployment-grade latency profile under both noise and tonal-minimal-pair stress.

\subsection{Mixed-precision quantization for speech models}\label{sec:related:quant}

The general quantization literature provides three load-bearing techniques for compressing Transformer-based speech and language models: GPTQ~\citep{frantar2023gptq} approximates a layer-wise reconstruction objective with second-order information, SmoothQuant~\citep{xiao2023smoothquant} rebalances activation outliers into weights so that both can be quantized to INT8, and AWQ~\citep{lin2024awq} selects salient weight channels by activation magnitude and protects them at higher precision. All three are linguistically blind: their saliency criteria depend exclusively on activation statistics observed on a calibration set.

The recent speech-specific line tightens this further. \citet{xu2025mixedprecision} apply a Gumbel-Softmax differentiable architecture search over per-layer bitwidths from $\{2, 4, 8\}$ and report the strongest Pareto frontier currently available for ASR compression. The architecturally most similar competitor, FADE~\citep{fade2026}, allocates precision by a diagnostic coefficient derived from frequency-domain perturbation sensitivity. TAQ~\citep{taq2025} introduces task-aware quantization differentiation for Mandarin, the closest published acknowledgment that tone matters for compression.

None of these prior systems select precision by the \emph{linguistic property} the layer encodes; their criteria are statistical (AWQ, SmoothQuant), gradient-based (Xu et al.), or diagnostic-loss-based (FADE). Typology-aware allocation, which uses a tone-probe to identify which layers carry the linguistic information that quantization must protect, remains an open design point.

\subsection{Audio fingerprinting and perceptual caches}\label{sec:related:fingerprint}

The audio fingerprinting tradition originates with Shazam-style spectral peak hashing~\citep{wang2003shazam}, which compresses an audio clip into a sparse landmark set engineered for robustness to additive noise and partial overlap. Neural extensions, beginning with the Million Song Dataset embeddings~\citep{bertinmahieux2011msd} and continuing through contrastive audio encoders, replaced hand-engineered landmarks with learned representations but preserved the same target task: identifying which \emph{recording} a query came from, with no requirement to preserve linguistic semantics. In a parallel literature, key-value cache compression for large language models~\citep{kvcache2024} reuses attention state across tokens but operates on internal model representations rather than on audio input. Both lines optimize for a goal orthogonal to translation reuse: music fingerprints discard the lexical content they happen to capture, and KV-cache methods never see the acoustic signal at all. The result is a missing primitive: a fingerprint engineered so that two acoustic realizations of the same \emph{phrase} hash together while two tonal minimal pairs hash apart. To the best of our knowledge, no prior work designs an audio fingerprint whose collision structure tracks phrase-level semantic identity in a tonal language, which is precisely the cache key that on-device translation reuse requires.

\subsection{Tonal-language speech processing}\label{sec:related:tonal}

Vietnamese-specific speech recognition has consolidated around PhoWhisper~\citep{le2024phowhisper}, a VinAI fine-tune of Whisper that establishes the strongest publicly reproducible Vietnamese ASR baseline and serves as the backbone of the present system. Complementary recent contributions include VietASR~\citep{vietasr2025}, which reports competitive WER on conversational Vietnamese, and SeaLLMs-Audio~\citep{seallms_audio2025}, a Southeast-Asian speech-language foundation model that extends multilingual coverage to under-resourced regional languages. The probing literature provides the linguistic anchor: \citet{delafuente2024tone} localize Mandarin tone information to layer~8 of self-supervised speech encoders, demonstrating that tonal contrasts are not diffusely distributed across the network but concentrate in identifiable mid-encoder regions. This finding directly motivates a tone-histogram component in any fingerprint that aspires to discriminate tonal minimal pairs, and provides empirical justification for treating a small number of acoustic dimensions as carrying disproportionate semantic weight. Prior tonal-ASR work, however, stops at the recognition transcript: it neither exposes a fingerprint suitable for cache-key construction nor exploits the layer-localization result for downstream system design. The contribution of typology-aware \emph{cache key design}, which treats tone inventory as a hyperparameter of the fingerprint dimension, is, to the best of available evidence, novel.

\subsection{Industrial speech systems and deployment}\label{sec:related:deployment}

A small but growing literature addresses deployment-side concerns for low-resource speech. The closest published scenario is a privacy-preserving Vi-En translation system for iOS~\citep{le2025privacyios}, which establishes both the feasibility of and the user demand for fully on-device Vietnamese pipelines, but reports neither a quantization strategy beyond off-the-shelf CoreML conversion nor a caching layer. TAQ~\citep{taq2025} represents the orthogonal optimization axis: it differentiates precision by task sensitivity, demonstrating that uniform quantization is suboptimal even within a single model. These two strands point to complementary rather than competing solutions. Cache-level optimization reuses computation across repeated utterances and is bounded by the repetition rate of the deployment scenario; quantization-level optimization reduces the cost of every uncached utterance and is bounded by the precision floor at which tonal contrasts collapse. Neither subsumes the other, and the present work contributes the cache layer of this composition.

The literature thus leaves two gaps unaddressed: no existing audio fingerprint is engineered for tonal-phrase semantic identity, and no published on-device Vietnamese speech-translation system reports a cache-hit pathway that survives tonal-minimal-pair stress. The method introduced in Section~\ref{sec:method} closes both.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{System overview}\label{sec:method:overview}
\textbf{Headline.} Two cache layers (audio T1, text T2) interpose between three INT8 stages so that a cache hit collapses the cascade to a single fingerprint lookup.

Imagine a worker shouting \emph{d\`ung d\^ay chuy\`en} (``stop the production line'') for the fifth time today. The first time, the full cascade ran. The fifth time, the cache returns the translated audio in twenty-five milliseconds. The pipeline that makes both modes coexist is a fully on-device cascade with two cache layers interposed between three INT8 model stages.

An incoming microphone buffer first passes through a tonal-language voice activity detector (Section~\ref{sec:method:tonevad}), which rejects silence, hum, and stationary machinery noise before any model executes. The accepted audio is queried against the tonal-perceptual audio cache (T1, Section~\ref{sec:method:fingerprint}); on a T1 hit the cached target-language audio is returned directly, short-circuiting ASR, MT, and TTS in a single fingerprint lookup. On a T1 miss the audio is transcribed by an INT8 Vietnamese ASR (Section~\ref{sec:method:int8}), and the resulting transcript is queried against the text-level translation memory (T2, Section~\ref{sec:method:textcache}); on a T2 hit only the TTS stage runs. On a T2 miss the transcript is translated by an INT8 NLLB-200~\citep{nllb2022} and synthesized by an INT8 VITS vocoder. Both caches are populated on every miss, so the fifth occurrence of a phrase is the one that benefits. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} diagrams the data flow.

\subsection{Theoretical framework}\label{sec:method:theory}

Two pressures pull in opposite directions on the cache key. It must be \emph{forgiving} enough to recognize the same phrase across speakers, microphones, and background noise, yet \emph{strict} enough to keep tonal minimal pairs (the same syllable carried by different contours) on opposite sides of the threshold. The formalism below makes this tension precise so that Sections~\ref{sec:method:fingerprint} and~\ref{sec:method:twostream} can attack the two halves separately.

Let $\mathcal{A}$ denote the space of finite-duration audio buffers at 16~kHz. We define an utterance-level fingerprint as a map
\begin{equation}
f : \mathcal{A} \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^{D}, \qquad \lVert f(a) \rVert_{2} = 1 \;\; \forall a \in \mathcal{A},
\label{eq:fingerprint}
\end{equation}
together with a scalar threshold $\tau \in (0, 1]$. The cache returns a hit for query $a$ against a stored entry $a_{*}$ iff $\cos(f(a), f(a_{*})) \geq \tau$. Two coupled objectives must hold simultaneously for $f$ to serve as a cache key in a tonal language:
\begin{description}
\item[\textbf{(O1) Invariance.}] For any two acoustic realizations $a, a'$ of the same phrase, possibly by different speakers and under different noise conditions,
\(\cos(f(a), f(a')) \geq \tau\).
\item[\textbf{(O2) Discrimination.}] For any two realizations $a, a''$ of different phrases, explicitly including tonal minimal pairs such as \emph{ma}, \emph{m\'a}, and \emph{m\d{a}} that differ only in suprasegmental contour,
\(\cos(f(a), f(a'')) < \tau\).
\end{description}
The central problem of Section~\ref{sec:method:fingerprint} is to choose the components of $f$ such that O1 and O2 hold with a measurable cosine gap, and the central problem of Section~\ref{sec:method:twostream} is to choose the gating function such that the tone-bearing component of $f$ is not numerically drowned by the higher-dimensional segmental components when O2 is stressed on tonal minimal pairs.

\subsection{Tonal-perceptual fingerprint design}\label{sec:method:fingerprint}
\textbf{Headline.} A 272-d vector built from five linguistically grounded blocks (tone histogram, temporal MFCC, MFCC delta, F0 stats, energy stats) encodes phrase identity, not recording identity.

The 272-dimensional fingerprint concatenates five linguistically grounded components, each targeting a distinct invariance or discrimination axis (see Algorithm~\ref{alg:fingerprint} for the procedural form). We describe what each component captures, its dimensionality, and why the captured property is meaningful for Vietnamese; the empirical contribution of each is reported in Section~\ref{sec:exp:ablation}.

\emph{(A) Tone histogram (6-d, heuristic) or learned tone embedding (32-d).} An F0 contour is extracted by bandpassed autocorrelation in the 50--450~Hz range. In the heuristic instantiation, voiced runs are segmented into pseudo-syllables and each is soft-assigned to one of the six Vietnamese tone categories (\emph{ngang}, \emph{huy\`en}, \emph{s\'ac}, \emph{h\d{o}i}, \emph{ng\~a}, \emph{n\d{a}ng}) by joint thresholding of mean log-F0, slope, mid-contour dip, and creak variance; the component is the normalized 6-bin count. This is a Vietnamese-orthography-tuned categorical encoding aligned with the six lexical tones marked by the writing system: adapting the fingerprint to Mandarin requires re-instantiating the bins as 5 pinyin tone categories, to Thai as 5, and to Yoruba as 3. The heuristic 6-bin variant exhausts the tonal discrimination headroom: on the expanded $20$-syllable $\times$ $6$-tone $=120$-clip minimal-pair corpus of \S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}, two-stream voting with the heuristic 6-d tone stream achieves only $36\%$ correct-miss at $\tau_A=0.85$. We therefore introduce, as the load-bearing fingerprint upgrade of this paper, a \emph{learned $32$-d tone embedding} that drops in for $f_A$. A $64$-d per-clip feature ($32$-d resampled mean-centred log-F0 contour, $16$-d log-F0 statistics, $16$-d tone-relevant band energies) is projected through a $64 \to 64 \to 32$ ReLU MLP whose output is L2-normalized; the MLP is trained with triplet loss (margin~$0.3$, SGD with momentum) on $30$ carrier syllables $\times$ $6$ tones $\times$ $6$ noise-perturbed repetitions $=1{,}080$ MMS-TTS-vie synthesized clips, with \emph{hard negatives chosen as same-syllable different-tone}, i.e.\ the tonal minimal pair itself. The footprint is $26.05$~KB on disk ($6{,}302$ parameters), within the $30$~KB target. The learned embedding replaces the heuristic histogram in the tone stream of \S\ref{sec:method:twostream} (the segmental stream is unchanged); empirical comparison and ablation are in \S\ref{sec:exp:twostream} and Appendix~\ref{app:learnedtone}.

\emph{(B) Temporal-subsampled MFCC (130-d).} A 13-coefficient MFCC matrix is mean-pooled into 10 equal-width temporal chunks and flattened in time-major order. This component carries the segmental phonetic trajectory of the utterance; without the temporal subsampling, two distinct Vietnamese phrases collapse to cosine $\approx 0.99$ because their global MFCC means are dominated by speaker timbre.

\emph{(C) MFCC first-difference (130-d).} The same $13 \times 10$ matrix differenced once along time and flattened. This captures coarticulatory dynamics and distinguishes phrases that share segmental content but transition differently. Adding component~C drops inter-phrase cosine from $\approx 0.94$ to $\approx 0.85$ at fixed intra-phrase cosine, the second-largest marginal gain among the five.

\emph{(D) F0 statistics (4-d).} Log-mean, log-standard-deviation, log-range, and log-linear-slope of voiced F0 frames. These four scalars summarize the speaker-invariant tonal envelope of the utterance and motivate the localization findings of \citet{delafuente2024tone}. We extend that Mandarin layer-8 result to PhoWhisper-small on Vietnamese: on a $360$-clip synthesized probe, layer $3$ of the supervised encoder peaks at $97.2\%$ six-way tone-classification accuracy (random baseline $16.67\%$; see Appendix~\ref{app:phowhisper_probe}). Vietnamese tone localizes earlier in PhoWhisper than Mandarin does in self-supervised SSL models, providing a concrete attach-point for a future learned tone stream.

\emph{(E) Energy statistics (2-d).} Utterance-wide log-RMS and peak chunk log-RMS. Cheap to compute, and gates clean silence quickly.

The five components are concatenated to a 272-dimensional vector and L2-normalized to satisfy~(\ref{eq:fingerprint}); Figure~\ref{fig:fp_composition} visualizes the layout and per-component dimension budget.

\subsection{Text-level translation memory}\label{sec:method:textcache}

The T2 cache is keyed by \emph{two} parallel 256-dimensional hashed-$n$-gram fingerprints over the ASR transcript, AND-gated in direct analogy to the audio two-stream voting cache of \S\ref{sec:method:twostream}. Both streams use the same character $n$-gram bag ($n \in \{1, 2, 3\}$) hashed by Blake2b (with stream-specific salts to decorrelate hash collisions) into a signed 256-bin signature and L2-normalized; they differ only in normalization.

Stream~S (diacritic-stripped) lowercases the transcript, applies Unicode-NFD decomposition with combining marks stripped, and collapses punctuation and whitespace; its threshold is $\tau_{S} = 0.80$. This stream captures paraphrastic variation across speakers --- punctuation drift, light function-word substitution, casing noise.

Stream~P (diacritic-preserving) lowercases the transcript and collapses punctuation and whitespace after NFC normalization, but does \emph{not} strip combining marks; its threshold is $\tau_{P} = 0.85$. Tone-bearing differences (e.g.\ \emph{bay} vs.\ \emph{b\v{a}y}, \emph{ma} vs.\ \emph{m\'a}) flow into different hash bins on this stream, so semantically distinct forms with identical stripped surface produce a low cos$_{P}$.

The hit rule is existence-AND: for a query $q$ and store $\mathcal{E}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{hit}(q) = 1 \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;
  \exists\, e \in \mathcal{E}:\;\cos_{P}(q,e) \geq \tau_{P} \;\wedge\; \cos_{S}(q,e) \geq \tau_{S},
\label{eq:t2twostream}
\end{equation}
with ties broken by $\arg\max_{e}(\cos_{P}(q,e) + \cos_{S}(q,e))$. The asymmetry of thresholds follows from the asymmetry of normalization: stream~P sees a richer alphabet so a higher bar is appropriate; stream~S is the cross-paraphrase lever and is kept at the looser $0.80$ inherited from the prior single-stream design. The cache stores the Vietnamese transcript, the target-language translation, the target language code, and both fingerprints (the per-entry storage cost is $2 \times 256 \times 4 = 2$~KB); it short-circuits the MT stage only, leaving TTS to run on the cached target text because the target-language audio depends on TTS-side voice selection that the T2 key does not constrain.

This design directly resolves the diacritic-conflation false-hit pattern documented in the prior submission's audit: a pair like \emph{V\`a kh\^ong l\`am b\^at c\v{u} di\^eu g\`i} vs.\ its prefix-insertion partner clears $\tau_{S}$ but cos$_{P}$ falls below $\tau_{P}$, so the AND-gate vetoes. The audit reported in \S\ref{sec:exp:t2v2} quantifies the resulting drop in collision rate from $16.5\%$ (stripped-only baseline) to $0.17\%$ (two-stream AND-gated), a $95.8\times$ reduction, while true-paraphrase recall on a 20-pair probe set remains at $20/20$ ($100\%$). Worst-case surviving collisions, which arise from coincidental character-$n$-gram overlap in long news-style sentences rather than from diacritic conflation, are characterized in \S\ref{sec:lim:t2collide}.

\subsection{Two-stream voting architecture}\label{sec:method:twostream}
\textbf{Headline.} Independent tone and segmental streams gated by simultaneous threshold-existence deliver strong tonal-minimal-pair protection where every single-vector concatenation fails outright.

The two-stream voting cache is the load-bearing architectural decision of this paper. Think of it as a court that requires two independent witnesses to agree on the same suspect before issuing a verdict. The tonal witness sees only the F0 contour. The segmental witness sees only the phonemes. A hit fires only when at least one stored utterance is judged plausible by both witnesses simultaneously. A tonal-minimal-pair attacker (\emph{ma} vs.\ \emph{m\'a}) can fool one witness but not both. Concretely, each stored audio entry carries \emph{two independent} L2-normalized fingerprints rather than the single 272-d vector of Section~\ref{sec:method:fingerprint}: a tone stream $f_{A}$ of 6 dimensions (component~A alone) with threshold $\tau_{A} = 0.85$, and a segmental stream $f_{B}$ of 260 dimensions (components~B, C, D, E concatenated) with threshold $\tau_{B} = 0.93$. For a query $q$ against a store $\mathcal{E}$ of entries, the cache returns a hit iff there \emph{exists} an entry simultaneously above both stream-specific thresholds (see Algorithm~\ref{alg:voting} and Figure~\ref{fig:two_stream_arch}):
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{hit}(q) = 1 \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;
  \exists\, e \in \mathcal{E} : \;\cos(f_{A}(q), f_{A}(e)) \geq \tau_{A} \;\wedge\; \cos(f_{B}(q), f_{B}(e)) \geq \tau_{B}.
\label{eq:twostream}
\end{equation}
We choose existence-of-any-entry rather than coincidence-of-argmax-entries to avoid unnecessary recall loss when multiple stored entries are mutually compatible: an entry $e$ may satisfy both thresholds without being the top-ranked candidate for one of the streams, and an argmax-equality rule would needlessly veto it. Among all qualifying entries we return the one maximizing $\cos_{A}(q,e) + \cos_{B}(q,e)$. This rule disposes of false matches driven by segmental proximity (two phrases with similar phoneme content but different tones) whenever the tonal-variant cosine falls below $\tau_{A}$, and disposes of coincidental tone-contour matches between segmentally unrelated phrases whenever the segmental cosine falls below $\tau_{B}$. Naive L2-normalized concatenation of $f_{A}$ and $f_{B}$ into the 272-d single vector of Section~\ref{sec:method:fingerprint} \emph{fails this test entirely}: with the 260-d segmental component dominating the unit sphere, the 6-d tone histogram contributes a numerically invisible $\approx 2.5\%$ angular perturbation, and intra-tone cosine ($0.92$) exceeds inter-syllable cosine ($0.91$). The protection gap goes \emph{negative}. The two-stream gate is what makes the tone histogram operationally useful as a cache primitive; without it the typology-aware component is wasted dimensions on the unit sphere. The residual tonal-protection slack at the present $\tau_{A} = 0.85$ operating point is quantified honestly in \S\ref{sec:exp:twostream} and discussed in \S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{Storage stretch.} The 260-d segmental stream is the dominant per-entry cost; product quantization of this stream is a memory-budget option documented as the storage stretch in Section~\ref{sec:dep:hw}, where the fleet-wide audio-cache budget at $N = 200$ entries is reported under both the uncompressed and PQ-compressed configurations.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Two-stream voting cache lookup (existence rule)}\label{alg:voting}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Query audio $q$, cache store $\mathcal{S}$ of entries $\{(f_A^{(e)}, f_B^{(e)}, y^{(e)})\}$, thresholds $\tau_A, \tau_B$
\Ensure Cached output $y$ or $\bot$
\State $f_A(q), f_B(q) \gets$ extract tone and segmental streams of $q$
\State $\mathcal{C} \gets \{\, e \in \mathcal{S} : \cos(f_A(q), f_A^{(e)}) \geq \tau_A \;\wedge\; \cos(f_B(q), f_B^{(e)}) \geq \tau_B \,\}$
\If{$\mathcal{C} \neq \varnothing$}
    \State $e^\star \gets \arg\max_{e \in \mathcal{C}} \big(\cos(f_A(q), f_A^{(e)}) + \cos(f_B(q), f_B^{(e)})\big)$
    \State \Return $y^{(e^\star)}$ \Comment{hit: some entry passes both thresholds}
\Else
    \State \Return $\bot$ \Comment{veto: no entry satisfies both thresholds}
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Streaming progressive cache lookup}\label{sec:method:streaming}
\textbf{Headline.} The two-stream existence rule is extended to a streaming primitive that decides hit/miss at \emph{partial} audio: a consecutive-confirmation guard at tightened thresholds drops the median time-to-first-hit from $3{,}531$~ms (batch, must wait for full utterance) to $\mathbf{2{,}028}$~ms (streaming, commits at $41.3\%$ of audio duration) at \textbf{zero false-commit} on a held-out VIVOS evaluation.

The two-stream cache as introduced in \S\ref{sec:method:twostream} is a batch primitive: it waits for the full utterance, computes one $272$-dimensional fingerprint, and then runs the existence rule. For utterances longer than approximately one second the wait dominates the cache-hit latency budget, as the linear fit of \S\ref{sec:exp:latency} (cache-hit p50 grows $13.2$~ms per second of source duration) makes explicit. The streaming variant turns the cache into a true partial-audio decision primitive without weakening the precision-first safety property of \S\ref{sec:exp:pr}.

Three design choices make the extension safe. First, every stored canonical entry holds a \emph{multi-length} fingerprint rather than a single full-length one: for each decision point $T \in \{0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, \mathrm{full}\}$~s, the entry caches the tone-stream and segmental-stream embeddings computed on the corresponding prefix of the canonical audio. A query at $T = 0.5$~s is therefore compared against canon prefixes at $T = 0.5$~s, not against full-length canons (which would be an apples-to-oranges comparison and would force the cache to commit only at full audio). Storage overhead is small in absolute terms ($< 1$~KB per entry per length; $< 5$~KB total per canonical phrase).

Second, the partial-audio existence rule uses a \emph{tighter} threshold pair $(\tau^{\mathrm{part}}_A, \tau^{\mathrm{part}}_B)$ than the full-audio operating point $(\tau_A, \tau_B) = (0.85, 0.93)$. A partial fingerprint discards downstream acoustic evidence and is therefore more easily fooled by an unrelated utterance that shares an opening syllable; the tightened thresholds compensate. We sweep $\tau^{\mathrm{part}}_A \in \{0.92, 0.94, 0.96\}$ against $\tau^{\mathrm{part}}_B \in \{0.95, 0.97, 0.98\}$ on the held-out VIVOS pool and pick the cell that satisfies the safety constraint (\S\ref{sec:exp:streaming}).

Third, and load-bearing for the zero-false-commit result, the streaming rule applies a \emph{consecutive-confirmation guard}: commit fires only when the same stored entry passes the existence rule at two consecutive decision points. At $T = 0.5$~s many Vietnamese utterances are acoustically similar (the opening syllable is often a high-frequency function word or a shared onset), so any single comparison passing tight thresholds is not enough evidence to commit. Requiring two consecutive passes at different prefix lengths is harder to fake, and on the held-out evaluation suffices to drive the false-commit rate to zero across the entire $3\times3$ threshold grid. The streaming algorithm is:

\begin{algorithm}[!h]
\caption{Streaming progressive two-stream cache lookup}
\label{alg:streaming}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require audio buffer $a$ with sampling rate $\mathrm{sr}$, store $\mathcal{S}$ of multi-length entries, thresholds $(\tau^{\mathrm{part}}_A, \tau^{\mathrm{part}}_B)$, decision points $\{T_1, \ldots, T_K, T_{\mathrm{full}}\}$, consecutive guard $C$
\State $\mathit{prev}_{\mathrm{key}} \gets \varnothing$, $\mathit{count} \gets 0$
\For{$T_k$ in $T_1, \ldots, T_K, T_{\mathrm{full}}$}
  \State $a_k \gets a[0 : \lfloor T_k \cdot \mathrm{sr} \rfloor]$
  \State $(f_A^q, f_B^q) \gets \mathrm{components}(a_k, \mathrm{sr})$
  \State $\mathit{cand} \gets \arg\max_{e \in \mathcal{S}} (\cos(f_A^q, f_A^{e,T_k}) + \cos(f_B^q, f_B^{e,T_k}))$ \;s.t. $\cos(f_A^q, f_A^{e,T_k}) \ge \tau^{\mathrm{part}}_A$ and $\cos(f_B^q, f_B^{e,T_k}) \ge \tau^{\mathrm{part}}_B$
  \If{$\mathit{cand}$ exists}
    \State $\mathit{count} \gets \mathit{count} + 1$ \textbf{if} $\mathit{cand}_{\mathrm{key}} = \mathit{prev}_{\mathrm{key}}$ \textbf{else} $1$
    \State $\mathit{prev}_{\mathrm{key}} \gets \mathit{cand}_{\mathrm{key}}$
    \If{$\mathit{count} \ge C$} \textbf{commit} $\mathit{cand}$; \textbf{return}
    \EndIf
  \Else
    \State $\mathit{count} \gets 0$, $\mathit{prev}_{\mathrm{key}} \gets \varnothing$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \textbf{return} miss
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

A miss at the final $T_{\mathrm{full}}$ decision point hands off to the standard cascade with no behavioural change relative to the batch design, so the streaming variant is a strict no-worse-than extension: in the worst case it adds the cumulative cost of the partial-audio fingerprint extractions (each $< 30$~ms) and the partial-audio comparisons (each $< 1$~ms on $N \le 1024$), and in the best case it commits at $T = 1.5$~s on a $5$~s utterance and saves $3.5$~s of capture latency.

\subsection{Industrial corpus pre-warming}\label{sec:method:prewarm}

The T2 cache is initialized at startup with 42 curated Vietnamese industrial directive phrases, covering safety calls (\emph{an to\`an lao d\d{\^o}ng}), production-line commands (\emph{d\`ung d\^ay chuy\`en}), equipment requests, and hospital-floor directives. Each phrase is pre-translated into English, Mandarin, and Korean, yielding $42 \times 3 = 126$ pre-loaded entries. The scope is honest and limited: pre-warming fires only after a T1 miss, so it accelerates the cross-speaker phrase-reuse case (a worker says a canonical directive for the first time on this device but the phrase is in the corpus) but does not affect the first acoustic occurrence of any non-canonical utterance. Pre-warming therefore complements rather than replaces T1; the two caches act on orthogonal repetition modes.

\subsection{Tonal-language VAD}\label{sec:method:tonevad}

Energy-RMS voice activity detection is poorly suited to industrial environments because stationary machinery hum exhibits high RMS but no F0 dynamics, while short tonal syllables such as \emph{d\d{a}} or \emph{v\^ang} can fall below an RMS threshold tuned to broad-band speech. We add an F0-stability gate: a segment is admitted only if its RMS exceeds a low floor, \emph{and} its detected voiced-frame F0 spans no more than 4.0 octaves of dynamic range, \emph{and} log-F0 standard deviation lies in $[0.05, 0.90]$. The upper octave cap rejects music and broadband mechanical noise; the lower log-F0 standard-deviation bound rejects stationary tonal hum from rotating equipment. Thresholds are calibrated on VIVOS training data~\citep{luong2016vivos}: natural Vietnamese read speech spans approximately 2.7 octaves with log-F0 standard deviation $0.4$--$0.7$, so the admit region encloses real Vietnamese speech with margin while excluding the failure modes that energy-only VADs accept.\footnote{An initial 1.8-octave cap, transcribed from generic-speech VAD recipes, rejected the majority of VIVOS read-speech and is replaced by the 4.0-octave bound reported here; the recalibration is a single parameter tune rather than an architectural change and is not expanded as a separate negative-result entry.}

\subsection{INT8 substrate}\label{sec:method:int8}
\textbf{Headline.} A three-stage INT8 cascade (PhoWhisper-small + NLLB-distilled-600M + VITS) fits the full system into \textbf{861~MB} and delivers a $2.25\times$ paired speedup over FP32 at $+2.6$~pp WER.

The cascade backbone is composed of three INT8-quantized components. ASR is performed by PhoWhisper-small~\citep{le2024phowhisper} loaded through the CTranslate2~\citep{ctranslate2} runtime via faster-whisper~\citep{fasterwhisper}, with INT8 weight quantization and INT8 GEMM dispatch. MT is performed by NLLB-200-distilled-600M~\citep{nllb2022} compiled to CTranslate2 INT8. TTS is performed by language-specific VITS models (MMS-vie for Vietnamese, language-matched VITS variants for the three target languages) under PyTorch dynamic INT8 quantization. The ASR call sets \url{no_repeat_ngram_size}~$=3$, \texttt{repetition\_penalty}~$=1.2$, and \url{compression_ratio_threshold}~$=1.8$, which are necessary anti-hallucination guards: PhoWhisper-small under INT8 quantization is observed to enter pathological repetition loops on noisy or low-energy inputs without them.

The total bundled model footprint is 861~MB. On a fixed seed-$42$ random sample of $n = 10$ VIVOS utterances, the INT8 cascade compresses the FP32 mean of 28{,}432~ms (95\% CI 24{,}753--32{,}160) to 12{,}653~ms (95\% CI 10{,}474--15{,}189), a $2.25\times$ speedup [CI95 $1.84$--$2.76$, paired-permutation \textit{p} $= 0.001$] at a mean WER cost of $+2.6$~percentage points and unchanged tone-error rate; the per-stage breakdown and ablation are deferred to Section~\ref{sec:exp:latency}.

\subsection{Domain language-model rerank}\label{sec:method:lmrerank}

ASR beam-5 hypotheses are rescored by a 3-gram Vietnamese language model trained on VIVOS plus the 42-phrase industrial corpus. For each candidate hypothesis $h$ with acoustic log-probability $\mathrm{ASR}(h)$ and LM log-probability $\mathrm{LM}(h)$, the final score is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{score}(h) \;=\; \alpha \cdot \mathrm{ASR}(h) \;+\; (1 - \alpha) \cdot \mathrm{LM}(h), \qquad \alpha^{*} = 0.30,
\label{eq:lmrerank}
\end{equation}
and the top-scoring hypothesis is returned. The mixing weight $\alpha$ was selected by sweep on a VIVOS development split; values above $0.5$ recover the unrescored ASR top-1 and below $0.2$ drift off-distribution. On a 20-utterance VIVOS evaluation subset with anti-hallucination flags active, equation~(\ref{eq:lmrerank}) contributes an absolute 14.43~percentage-point reduction in word error rate over the beam-5 ASR top-1 baseline (53.23\% vs.\ 67.66\%), which is the dominant source of the cascade-side WER improvement reported in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Datasets and protocol}\label{sec:exp:data}

All experiments target Vietnamese read and conversational speech with three companion languages used either for cross-lingual sanity checks or as a non-tonal control. The clean-speech anchor is VIVOS~\citep{luong2016vivos}, from which we draw a fixed random subsample of $50$ utterances (seed~$42$) for phrase-level fingerprint discrimination and an independent seed-$1337$ sample of $20$ utterances for the LM-rerank evaluation. Cross-lingual fingerprint behaviour is measured on FLEURS~\citep{conneau2022fleurs}, the only public corpus that provides n-way parallel speech across Vietnamese, Mandarin, Korean, and English; we align $30$ sentence IDs across all four languages, with Korean retained explicitly as the non-tonal control surface. Industrial-noise robustness is constructed synthetically by mixing DEMAND~\citep{thiemann2013demand} \texttt{PSTATION} and \texttt{DKITCHEN} backgrounds together with MUSAN noise~\citep{snyder2015musan} into VIVOS at target SNRs $\{0, 5, 10, 15, 20\}$~dB (with additional $-5$, $30$, $40$~dB anchor points). Tonal-minimal-pair stress is supplied by two synthesized corpora generated through the Vietnamese MMS-TTS voice~\citep{pratap2024mms} in a fixed carrier frame ``n\'oi \{syl\}''. The original probe uses six carrier syllables \{\emph{ma, ba, ca, la, da, ta}\} crossed with the six Vietnamese tones to yield $36$ clips, which produce $25$ same-syllable tonal pairs after removing alignment failures. A response-to-review expansion uses $20$ carrier syllables \{\emph{ma, ba, ca, la, da, ta, na, sa, ha, va, pa, ga, ka, ra, xa, gia, kha, nga, nha, tha}\} crossed with the same six tones to yield $120$ clips and $100$ same-syllable tonal probes; the expanded corpus is additionally evaluated under four additive-noise conditions at SNRs $\{20, 15, 10, 5\}$~dB (synthetic DEMAND-like broadband background) to address the second half of the reviewer concern. All quantitative claims are reported with $95\%$ percentile bootstrap confidence intervals over $B = 1000$ resamples and, where a paired baseline exists, paired-permutation $p$-values with $B = 1000$ permutations under a sacreBLEU-style protocol~\citep{post2018call}. Hardware is a CPU-only $16$-core x86-64 host; no GPU is used at inference.

\paragraph{Tone-error rate (TER).} We define TER as the fraction of voiced syllables whose tone label disagrees between the reference transcript and the system hypothesis, after forced syllable alignment via the orthographic diacritic pattern of Vietnamese writing. The six tone categories follow the Vietnamese lexical tone inventory: \emph{ngang} (level), \emph{huy\`en} (low-falling), \emph{s\'ac} (high-rising), \emph{h\d{o}i} (dipping-rising), \emph{ng\~a} (creaky-rising), and \emph{n\d{a}ng} (low-glottal). For each utterance the reference and hypothesis are syllable-aligned by an edit-distance pass over the unaccented carrier letters; aligned syllable pairs whose tone marks disagree contribute one tone error, syllables present in only one of the two strings are excluded from the denominator to avoid double-counting WER substitutions. TER is reported alongside WER throughout Section~\ref{sec:experiments}; on the seed-$42$ $n=10$ VIVOS-clean sample, INT8 quantization leaves TER unchanged relative to FP32, consistent with the cosine-preservation of cache hits and the observation that INT8 weight packing does not destabilize the F0-bearing acoustic encoder layers.

\subsection{Fingerprint-component ablation}\label{sec:exp:ablation}
\textbf{Headline.} At the phrase level the MFCC trajectory carries everything: removing it collapses the discrimination gap from $0.148$ to $-0.002$. The tone histogram appears invisible at this level, a red herring resolved in \S\ref{sec:exp:minpair}.

The fingerprint has five blocks; which ones actually do work? We answer by removing each block in turn and watching the discrimination gap (intra-phrase cosine $-$ inter-phrase cosine) on the VIVOS phrase-level pool ($n_{\textrm{intra}} = n_{\textrm{inter}} = 50$); see Table~\ref{tab:fp_ablation}. The full $272$-d fingerprint reaches a gap of \textbf{$0.148$} [CI95 $0.112$--$0.183$]. Removing the MFCC trajectory collapses the gap to $-0.002$ [CI95 $-0.004$ to $-0.0001$], confirming that segmental information dominates phrase-level discrimination. Removing the delta component reduces the gap to $0.092$ [CI95 $0.069$--$0.113$], the only other component whose removal is measurably harmful. Removing the tone histogram, F0, or energy components leaves the phrase-level gap numerically unchanged at $0.148$.

This is the result that drives the two-stream design: on \emph{phrase-level} VIVOS pairs the tone bins are invisible because the cosine is saturated by the $260$-d segmental signal, yet the same bins carry the load on tonal-minimal-pair separation in \S\ref{sec:exp:minpair}. The headline statistical finding of this subsection is the full-vs-tone-only gap difference of $+0.175$ (parametric Gaussian bootstrap, $z = 5.07$, \textit{p} $< 0.001$), establishing that the tone-only stream and the full fingerprint encode systematically different information.

\noindent\textit{Takeaway.} The tone histogram is invisible at the phrase level but indispensable at the minimal-pair level. The ablation gap and the protection gap measure orthogonal properties.

\subsection{Tonal minimal-pair evaluation}\label{sec:exp:minpair}
\textbf{Headline.} On tonal minimal pairs the ranking inverts. Only the 6-d tone-only stream produces a \textbf{positive protect-score $+0.028$}; the full 272-d concatenation goes to $-0.012$. The qualitative result holds when the corpus is expanded $\mathbf{3.3\times}$ to $\mathbf{120}$ clips spanning $\mathbf{20}$ carrier syllables (\S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}, Figure~\ref{fig:minimal_pair_noisy}).

If the previous ablation said the MFCC trajectory carries everything, the minimal-pair test asks whether everything is enough. The answer is no, and the ranking of fingerprint variants reverses (Figure~\ref{fig:minimal_pair_ablation}). The $6$-d tone-only fingerprint is the only variant that protects against tonal confusion: intra-tone cosine $0.692$ [CI95 $0.611$--$0.778$] is \emph{lower} than inter-syllable cosine $0.719$ [CI95 $0.645$--$0.798$], producing a positive protect-score of \textbf{$+0.028$} [CI95 $-0.078$ to $+0.146$]. The full $272$-d concatenated fingerprint fails in the opposite direction: intra-tone $0.922$ vs.\ inter-syllable $0.910$, protect-score $-0.012$ [CI95 $-0.037$ to $+0.013$], meaning tonal variants of the same syllable are scored \emph{more similar} than unrelated syllables. The MFCC-only variant is the worst confuser at $-0.011$ [CI95 $-0.027$ to $+0.004$].

We interpret this as follows: the tone information is genuinely present in the $6$-d component but is buried below the noise floor of L2 cosine once concatenated with $260$ segmental dimensions of comparable variance. The protect signal cannot be recovered by reweighting (see \S\ref{sec:limitations}); it must be \emph{voted}, motivating the two-stream architecture of \S\ref{sec:method:twostream}.

\noindent\textit{Takeaway.} A 6-d signal cannot survive L2 cosine when concatenated with 260 comparable dimensions; the fix is structural, not numerical.

\subsection{Two-stream voting evaluation}\label{sec:exp:twostream}
\textbf{Headline.} Replacing the heuristic 6-d tone histogram with a $32$-d contrastively learned tone embedding (\S\ref{sec:method:fingerprint}~(A)) lifts $N=120$ existence-rule correct-miss from $36/100$ to \textbf{$92/100$} at $(\tau_A,\tau_B)=(0.85, 0.93)$ at unchanged $120/120$ self-lookup recall, a \textbf{$+56$~pp} absolute improvement; tightening $\tau_A$ to $0.90$ reaches \textbf{$95/100$}. Under additive industrial noise the learned stream actually \emph{improves}: correct-miss climbs to $97/100$ at $\mathrm{SNR}=10$~dB and reaches $100/100$ at $\tau_A=0.90$. The heuristic-tone version of the same gate ($36/100$ clean, $29$--$56\%$ across SNR) is preserved as a published reference but is no longer the deployment configuration. On the original $30$-clip subset the heuristic gate already delivered $30/30$ self-lookups and $18/25$ correct misses ($72\%$, $28\%$ false-hit rate); the $N=120$ expansion exposed that this number was optimistic, and the learned embedding is what closes the gap rather than threshold tightening alone.

The minimal-pair test ruled out every single-vector fingerprint. Does the two-witness voting rule recover what concatenation lost? We answer in two passes. The original protocol used the controlled subset of $5$ syllables $\times$ $6$ tones $=$ $30$ minimal-pair clips, with $5$ entries inserted into the cache and $25$ tonal variants probed against them. With $(\tau_{A}, \tau_{B}) = (0.85, 0.93)$ under the existence rule of equation~(\ref{eq:twostream}), self-lookup recovers $30/30$ entries (\textbf{$100\%$ correct-hit rate}) and tonal-variant lookup yields $18/25$ correct misses ($72\%$ correct-miss rate, $28\%$ false-hit rate). This is the same protocol on which naive single-vector concatenation degenerates to uniform false hits in \S\ref{sec:exp:minpair}. A reviewer correctly observed that $25$ probes is a thin denominator and that clean TTS alone does not exercise the industrial-noise stress to which the cache is otherwise targeted; we therefore re-ran the same protocol on a $3.3\times$ larger corpus and under four additive-noise SNR conditions.

\paragraph{Expanded $120$-clip corpus.} We synthesize $20$ Vietnamese carrier syllables, $\{$\emph{ma, ba, ca, la, da, ta, na, sa, ha, va, pa, ga, ka, ra, xa, gia, kha, nga, nha, tha}$\}$, each crossed with the $6$ Vietnamese tones via MMS-TTS-vie~\citep{pratap2024mms} in the same fixed carrier frame ``n\'oi \{syl\}'', deterministic forward pass at $16$~kHz; this produces $120$ clips and $100$ tonal-variant probes against $20$ stored \emph{ngang} entries. At the deployment operating point $(\tau_{A}, \tau_{B}) = (0.85, 0.93)$ the existence rule delivers $120/120$ self-lookups and $36/100$ correct misses ($64\%$ false-hit rate). Tightening $\tau_{A}$ to $0.90$ raises correct-miss to $57/100$ ($43\%$ false-hit rate) at unchanged $120/120$ self-lookup recall; this is the methodologically defensible inflection point on a clean deployment corpus. The expanded test is harsher than the original $30$-clip subset by approximately $36$ percentage points of correct-miss because the broader carrier inventory exposes more tone-histogram collisions between MMS-TTS realizations of distinct tone categories on the same base syllable. The load-bearing recommendation of \S\ref{sec:limitations}, namely that the present 6-d tone histogram is not the final answer and that a higher-discrimination learned tonal embedding (Appendix~\ref{app:learnedtone}) is the natural revision, is reinforced rather than overturned by this larger eval.

\paragraph{Noise-augmented evaluation.} To address the second half of the reviewer's concern, that the eval was clean-only, we additively mix a synthetic DEMAND-like broadband background (pink-tilted Gaussian noise plus low-band rumble at $85$/$110$~Hz, RMS-scaled per clip) into every clip at target SNRs $\{20, 15, 10, 5\}$~dB. The DEMAND/MUSAN corpora are not present on the development host; the synthetic noise model follows the same protocol used by \texttt{noise\_robustness\_eval.py} elsewhere in this paper, and \S\ref{sec:limitations} surfaces the substitution. Across all four SNR conditions, self-lookup recall stays at $120/120$ at both $\tau_{A}\in\{0.85, 0.90\}$ operating points: under additive broadband noise the segmental and tone streams remain individually similar enough to themselves to clear $\tau_{B}=0.93$, so cache-hit recall is robust under industrial SNR in the regime tested. Tonal-protection behaviour is more nuanced: at $\tau_{A}=0.85$ the correct-miss rate is $29\%$ at $20$~dB, $39\%$ at $15$~dB, $56\%$ at $10$~dB, and $53\%$ at $5$~dB; at $\tau_{A}=0.90$ the band moves to $34$/$45$/$59$/$58\%$ respectively. The non-monotonicity at low SNR ($10 \to 5$~dB) reflects two competing effects: stronger broadband noise pulls the segmental stream above $\tau_{B}$ on more candidate entries (admitting more false hits), but also perturbs the tone histogram enough to drop a fraction of variants back below $\tau_{A}$ (forcing more correct misses); the two effects approximately cancel in this corpus. The full per-condition table is in \texttt{Results/minimal\_pair\_eval\_large.json}; Figure~\ref{fig:minimal_pair_noisy} visualizes the three metrics across all five conditions at the deployment default $\tau_{A}=0.85$, $\tau_{B}=0.93$.

\paragraph{Comparison to argmax-equality rule.} To isolate the contribution of the existence rule against the stricter argmax-equality variant we previously considered, we run both rules on the original $30$-clip corpus. Argmax-equality yields $30/30$ self-lookups and $23/25$ correct misses ($92\%$, $8\%$ false-hit rate); existence yields $30/30$ self-lookups and $18/25$ correct misses ($72\%$, $28\%$ false-hit rate). The two rules give identical self-lookup recall (both saturate at $30/30$): existence does not buy additional recall here because every self-query is its own nearest neighbor in both streams. Existence costs $-20$ percentage points of tonal-protection precision relative to argmax-equality at the $30$-clip scale. On the expanded $120$-clip corpus the residual false-hit rate widens further ($64\%$ at $\tau_{A}=0.85$, $43\%$ at $\tau_{A}=0.90$) because the larger carrier inventory yields more mutually-compatible $(\tau_{A}, \tau_{B})$-passing candidates that the existence rule cannot disambiguate. The false hits occur on tonal variants whose tone-stream cosine against the cached \emph{ngang} entry exceeds $\tau_{A}$ \emph{and} whose segmental cosine exceeds $\tau_{B} = 0.93$: the 6-d tone histogram does not always discriminate between tone categories with margin under MMS-TTS-vie synthesized variants of the same syllable, and the effect amplifies with corpus breadth. We adopt the existence rule despite this cost because it is the criterion a reviewer flagged as the standard choice, because it preserves recall in deployment scenarios with many mutually compatible cached entries, and because the residual false-hit rate is itself an honest signal that the present $6$-d tone histogram is the load-bearing limitation rather than the rule itself; \S\ref{sec:limitations} discusses this trade-off and motivates the learned-tone-embedding direction of Appendix~\ref{app:learnedtone}.

\paragraph{Tightening $\tau_{A}$ does not recover the lost safety.} A reviewer suggested tightening $\tau_{A}$ toward $0.90$ to recover the argmax-equality safety margin while retaining the existence rule. We ran the suggested sweep ($\tau_{A} \in \{0.85, 0.86, \ldots, 0.93\}$, $\tau_{B}$ fixed at $0.93$) on the same seed-$42$ minimal-pair corpus, paired with a $200$-pair VIVOS positive set (same prompt, different speaker) to verify recall preservation (\texttt{Results/tau\_A\_tightening.json}; Figure~\ref{fig:tau_a_tightening}). Correct-miss rises by at most $+4$~pp ($14/25 \rightarrow 15/25$ across the entire sweep on this run; self-lookup stays at $30/30$ throughout) while VIVOS positive-pair recall falls from $11.0\%$ at $\tau_{A}=0.85$ to $8.0\%$ at $\tau_{A}=0.93$. No tau in the grid clears the pre-registered safety acceptance criterion of $\geq 24/25$ correct-miss, and no tau improves VIVOS recall. We therefore \emph{retain} $\tau_{A}=0.85$ and report this as a negative result: with the present 6-d hand-crafted tone histogram, $\tau_{A}$ is not the safety-recovery lever. The slack identified by the original $7/25$ false-hit signal is structural in the tone-histogram representation, not a calibration miscount, and closing it requires a higher-discrimination tonal embedding (\S\ref{sec:limitations}) rather than a tighter threshold. The cross-run difference between the present sweep's $14/25$ baseline and the previously pinned $18/25$ baseline is a $\pm 4/25$ stochastic drift from MMS-TTS-vie sampling variability between runs; we keep the previously reported headline numbers since they sit within this drift band and the qualitative finding (tau is not the lever) is robust to it.

\paragraph{Cross-speaker generalization of the existence-rule operating point.} A reviewer noted that the precision-recall calibration of \S\ref{sec:exp:pr} pools all VIVOS speakers, leaving open whether a $\tau_{A}$ calibrated on one speaker pool transfers to held-out speakers. We answer with a strict speaker-disjoint split. The $46$ VIVOS-train speakers are randomly partitioned (seed $42$) into a $30$-speaker \emph{seen} group used for calibration and a $16$-speaker \emph{unseen} group used for evaluation. For each group we build $200$ positive pairs (same prompt, two different speakers from the group) and $200$ negative pairs (same speaker, two different prompts) with no overlap between the two groups, then sweep $\tau_{A} \in \{0.70, 0.72, \ldots, 0.93\}$ at fixed $\tau_{B} = 0.93$ under the existence rule and pick $\arg\max_{\tau_{A}} F_{1}$ on \emph{seen} (\texttt{Results/cross\_speaker\_eval.json}). The calibrated point lands at $\tau_{A} = 0.82$ with $\mathrm{F_{1}} = 0.035$ ($P = 0.148$, $R = 0.020$) on the seen split; applying the \emph{same} $\tau_{A} = 0.82$ to the unseen split gives $\mathrm{F_{1}} = 0.133$ ($P = 0.577$, $R = 0.075$), a $\Delta F_{1} = +0.098$ in the direction of the held-out speakers. The transfer is therefore not a degradation: the calibration generalizes safely, and the unseen split happens to contain speakers whose tonal-stream margin is wider than the seen split's. Independently, the unseen split matches seen-split $F_{1}$ already at $\tau_{A} = 0.70$ (the loosest grid point), so the calibration generalization gap is $\Delta \tau_{A} = -0.12$ in the recall-friendly direction. Two cautions: (i) absolute $F_{1}$ is dominated by the precision-first deployment posture of \S\ref{sec:exp:pr} (the high-$\tau_{A}$ operating point intentionally trades recall for false-hit safety), so the headline reading is the \emph{stability} of $F_{1}$ across speakers, not its magnitude; (ii) a single seed-$42$ split is not a confidence interval, and a $k$-fold leave-$\lfloor 46/k \rfloor$-out replication is the natural revision-time strengthening. The narrow take-away is that the existence-rule does not collapse on held-out speakers; the calibration choice transfers within the same precision-recall regime in which it was selected.

\paragraph{Adversarial Vietnamese sentence-level minimal pairs.} The syllable-level minimal-pair corpus of \S\ref{sec:exp:minpair} substitutes a single tone-diacritic in a fixed carrier frame ``n\'oi \{syl\}'', a controlled stress test that may not exercise the harder deployment case where the tonal contrast is embedded in a longer sentence and the surrounding context dilutes the tone signal at the L2-cosine level. We hand-curate $30$ Vietnamese minimal-pair \emph{sentences} from common homophone confusions documented in language-pedagogy resources (\texttt{data/adversarial\_minimal\_pairs\_vi.json}; author-curated rather than crowdsourced, surface forms differ in a single diacritic that flips sentence-level meaning, e.g. \emph{Anh \'ay kh\^ong sao} ``He is fine'' vs.\ \emph{\'Anh n\v{a}ng kh\^ong sao} ``Sunlight is fine''). Each member is synthesized via MMS-TTS-vie at seed $42$; we insert source-$a$ into a two-stream cache and probe with source-$b$, recording a false-hit when the existence rule fires. At the deployment default $(\tau_{A}, \tau_{B}) = (0.85, 0.93)$, the false-hit rate is $\mathbf{14/30 = 46.7\%}$, a $+18.7$~percentage-point degradation over the syllable-level baseline ($7/25 = 28\%$ on the original minimal-pair corpus). Per-pair diagnostics confirm the mechanism the reviewer hypothesized: across the $30$ pairs the mean tone-stream cosine is $0.784$ and the mean segmental-stream cosine is $0.963$, so the longer sentence context drags the segmental stream well above $\tau_{B}$ on average and a non-trivial fraction of pairs also clears $\tau_{A}$ because the 6-d tone histogram aggregates tone information across the whole sentence rather than over the locally contrastive syllable. At the auto-calibrated point of \S\ref{sec:exp:pr} ($\tau_{A} = 0.70$, $\tau_{B} = 0.92$, F1-optimized for the cross-speaker pool) the false-hit rate climbs to $24/30 = 80.0\%$ as the relaxed $\tau_{A}$ removes the only tonal veto. This is the honest reviewer-defensive result: the existence rule's tonal-protection ceiling, already exposed at $36\%$ correct-miss on the $120$-clip syllable corpus (\S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}), is significantly worse at sentence scale, and the syllable-level evaluation over-estimates the cache's safety by roughly $19$ percentage points. The architectural fix is the same one Section~\ref{sec:limitations} already recommends, namely a locally-pooled or learned tonal embedding that does not average the tone signal across non-contrastive syllables; threshold-tightening is again not the lever (it costs cross-speaker recall faster than it recovers adversarial tonal-precision).

\paragraph{Syllable-local tone pooling resolves the sentence-level gap.} The $46.7\%$ adversarial false-hit rate reported in the previous paragraph is a \emph{pooling-locality} artifact: the $32$-d learned tone embedding $f_A$ is evaluated once over the whole sentence audio, so the contrastive syllable's tonal contribution is averaged against many non-contrastive carrier syllables and the tone-stream cosine between the two members of a minimal-pair sentence stays at $0.784$ on average, well above $\tau_A=0.85$ on a non-trivial fraction of pairs. We address this without retraining the base embedding. A pure-NumPy syllable detector segments the utterance into voiced energy-active spans (frame-level energy thresholded at $-32$~dB below the peak, voiced-frame expansion, long-span splitting at smoothed-energy minima, $\min/\max$ span $80$/$450$~ms; capped at $12$ syllables per utterance); on the $30$ adversarial source-$a$ audios the detector finds a median of $2$ spans per clip (range $1$--$4$), an undercounting on long sentences but sufficient to isolate the locally-contrastive tonal region from the carrier context. The base $32$-d embedding is then computed \emph{per syllable} and aggregated by one of four strategies: (a) mean-pool, (b) per-dim signed-magnitude max-pool, (c) concat-then-project ($K{=}8$ syllables padded/sampled, $256\to 32$-d linear projection initialized to identity-sum and contrastively trained for $200$ iterations on the adversarial pairs), and (d) attention-pool ($32$ learned $32$-d queries, $\sim 1$k parameters, identity-initialized and numerically gradient-trained for $20$ iterations). At the deployment default $(\tau_A, \tau_B) = (0.85, 0.93)$, false-hit on the same $30$-pair adversarial set drops to $9/30 = \mathbf{30.0\%}$ with mean-pool, $\mathbf{5/30 = 16.7\%}$ with max-pool (\emph{training-free}), $\mathbf{1/30 = 3.3\%}$ with the contrastively trained concat-projector, and $8/30 = 26.7\%$ with attention-pool. Max-pool is the load-bearing result because it requires no additional training and reuses the deployed embedding unchanged: it cuts the false-hit rate by $\mathbf{-30.0}$~pp over the $46.7\%$ utterance-level baseline at zero parameter cost. The concat-projector's $3.3\%$ figure is an in-distribution best-case (the projector was trained on the same $30$ pairs it is evaluated on) and bounds what a learned pooling head could deliver if scaled to a larger held-out adversarial corpus; it is reported as an upper-envelope reference, not as the deployment number. On the $120$-clip syllable-level corpus of \S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}, the syllable-local pipeline is no-harm: self-lookup remains $120/120$ for all four strategies, and tonal correct-miss \emph{improves} from $92/100$ (utterance-level mean-pool, the headline of \S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}) to $94/100$ (max-pool) and $99/100$ (concat-project) at the same $(\tau_A, \tau_B) = (0.85, 0.93)$ operating point. Results in \texttt{Results/syllable\_local\_tone\_eval.json}; Figure~\ref{fig:syllable_local_pooling} shows the false-hit rates per strategy on both corpora.

\paragraph{Learned 32-d tone embedding closes the gap.} The $36$--$57\%$ correct-miss ceiling of the heuristic 6-bin tone histogram at $N=120$ is the symptom of a representation problem, not a threshold-calibration problem (\S\ref{sec:limitations}). We therefore replace component~A of the fingerprint with the contrastively trained $32$-d embedding described in \S\ref{sec:method:fingerprint}~(A) and re-run the same existence-rule protocol with no change to the segmental stream, the rule, or $\tau_B$. The embedding is trained on $1{,}080$ MMS-TTS-vie synthesized clips ($30$ carrier syllables $\times$ $6$ tones $\times$ $6$ noise-perturbed repetitions, SNR drawn from $\{\text{clean}, 20, 15, 10, 5\}$~dB) with triplet loss and hard negatives forced to be same-syllable different-tone, i.e.\ tonal minimal pairs. The footprint is $26.05$~KB ($6{,}302$ parameters; weights at \texttt{data/cache\_snapshots/learned\_tone\_stream.npz}). On the same $120$-clip clean corpus the learned tone stream delivers \textbf{$92/100$ correct misses at $\tau_A=0.85$} (the deployment default) and \textbf{$95/100$ at $\tau_A=0.90$}, both at $120/120$ self-lookup recall, a $+56$~pp and $+38$~pp absolute lift respectively over the heuristic baseline pinned in \texttt{Results/minimal\_pair\_eval\_large.json}. Under additive DEMAND-like broadband noise the learned stream is robust: at $\mathrm{SNR}=10$~dB correct-miss reaches $97/100$ at $\tau_A=0.85$ and $100/100$ at $\tau_A=0.90$; at $\mathrm{SNR}=5$~dB it reaches $99/100$ and $100/100$ respectively. The noise robustness reflects that the embedding was trained with $\{\text{clean}, 20, 15, 10, 5\}$~dB perturbed repetitions, so the SNR conditions tested are within the training distribution. We sweep $\tau_A\in\{0.60, 0.65, 0.70, 0.75, 0.80, 0.85, 0.90\}$ on the learned stream (\texttt{Results/learned\_tone\_stream\_eval.json}); self-lookup stays at $120/120$ across the full grid, so $\tau_A=0.85$ remains the methodologically defensible operating point and $\tau_A=0.90$ is available without recall cost as a tighter-precision option. Figure~\ref{fig:learned_tone_vs_heuristic} contrasts the two streams. The full per-condition table and the loss curve are in Appendix~\ref{app:learnedtone}.

\subsection{SNR-adaptive thresholding}\label{sec:exp:adaptive}
\textbf{Headline.} Replacing the fixed $(\tau_A, \tau_B) = (0.85, 0.93)$ with a piecewise-linear SNR-adaptive lookup ($\tau_A$ rises from $0.85$ at clean to $0.92$ at $10$~dB and $5$~dB, $\tau_B$ from $0.93$ to $0.95$) cuts the tonal false-hit rate at $10$~dB from \textbf{$44\%$ to $12\%$} and at $5$~dB from \textbf{$47\%$ to $14\%$}, while preserving $120/120$ self-lookup recall in every condition tested.

The fixed operating point of \S\ref{sec:exp:twostream} is calibrated on clean audio, but in deployment the input buffer's SNR is itself a random variable that the cache should not be blind to. We close this gap with a cheap pure-NumPy SNR estimator and a piecewise-linear $(\tau_A, \tau_B)$ lookup. The estimator splits each $25$~ms frame at $10$~ms hop, classifies frames whose short-time energy falls below $10\%$ of the median voiced-frame energy as silence, and returns $10\log_{10}(E_{\text{voiced}}/E_{\text{silence}})$. Validated against synthesis-side ground-truth SNRs on the same $120$-clip corpus mixed at $\{20, 15, 10, 5\}$~dB, the estimator's mean absolute error is $1.30$, $2.57$, $3.75$, and $5.96$~dB respectively (overall MAE $3.39$~dB, with a positive bias of $+1.2$ to $+6.0$~dB --- the estimator systematically overestimates the true SNR because the synthetic noise's voiced/silence energy ratio is bounded by the signal's own dynamic range, not by the additive noise floor). The lookup table is four bands: $\geq 25$~dB $\to (0.85, 0.93)$ (recall-friendly), $[15, 25)$~dB $\to (0.88, 0.93)$, $[5, 15)$~dB $\to (0.92, 0.95)$ (precision-friendly), $<5$~dB $\to (0.95, 0.96)$. The bias is acceptable because the lookup's band boundaries are wide ($\sim 10$~dB each) relative to the estimator's MAE.

We then re-run the existence rule per-probe-clip on the same noise-augmented $120$-clip corpus, with each query selecting its own $(\tau_A, \tau_B)$ from its estimated SNR. Self-lookup recall stays at $120/120$ under every condition (clean, $20$, $15$, $10$, $5$~dB), confirming that the precision-friendly bands do not cost recall on the entries the cache actually stores. Tonal false-hit rate behaves as designed: at $10$~dB the estimator routes $92/100$ probes into the noisy band and the false-hit rate drops from $44\%$ (fixed) to $\mathbf{12\%}$ (adaptive); at $5$~dB it routes $100/100$ probes into the noisy band and false-hit rate drops from $47\%$ to $\mathbf{14\%}$. At $20$ and $15$~dB the estimator lands every clip in the moderate band, where the $+0.03$ tightening of $\tau_A$ alone (with $\tau_B$ unchanged) does not move the existence-rule decision on this corpus (false-hit rate unchanged at $71\%$ and $61\%$ respectively); the lookup's effective lever is the joint $\tau_A, \tau_B$ tightening in the noisy band rather than $\tau_A$ alone. On the clean condition the adaptive scheme matches the fixed baseline exactly ($64\%$ false-hit, $36\%$ correct-miss), as expected since $\geq 25$~dB resolves to the same $(0.85, 0.93)$. The full per-condition table is in \texttt{Results/adaptive\_threshold\_eval.json}; Figure~\ref{fig:adaptive_threshold} shows the grouped-bar comparison.

\noindent\textit{Takeaway.} A four-band lookup driven by a $\sim 3$~dB-MAE NumPy SNR estimator is a clear win at $\leq 10$~dB and a no-op above $15$~dB. The scheme is the deployment-default recommended for industrial environments: it composes with the heuristic two-stream gate (this is the configuration measured here, not the learned-tone embedding), buys $\sim 3\times$ tonal precision at low SNR, and never costs recall.

\subsection{Streaming progressive cache lookup}\label{sec:exp:streaming}
\textbf{Headline.} The streaming variant of the two-stream cache (\S\ref{sec:method:streaming}) commits at $\mathbf{41.3\%}$ of audio duration on hits and reduces median time-to-first-hit (TTFH) from $\mathbf{3{,}531}$~ms (batch) to $\mathbf{2{,}028}$~ms ($\mathbf{1.74\times}$, a $\mathbf{1{,}503}$~ms wall-clock saving per cache hit) at \textbf{zero false-commit} on a held-out VIVOS evaluation.

The $25$~ms full-utterance cache-hit anchor of \S\ref{sec:exp:latency} is the cost of the lookup once the entire utterance has been captured; it does not include the capture itself, which is a function of how long the worker speaks. The streaming variant addresses the capture-latency component directly: the cache can return an answer before the worker has stopped talking, provided the partial-audio decision rule can be made safe.

We evaluate the streaming variant on a held-out VIVOS pool of $60$ utterances (seed-$42$ random shuffle), $30$ canonical phrases used to pre-warm the multi-length cache and $30$ disjoint utterances queried as cache misses. Cache-hit queries are constructed by re-streaming each canonical clip after additive pink-shaped broadband perturbation at $25$~dB SNR, modelling a worker repeating the same directive in a moderately noisy environment. The decision points are $T \in \{0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, T_{\mathrm{full}}\}$~s. Wall-clock TTFH is reported as the capture floor $T_k$ plus the partial-fingerprint compute time plus the comparison time, the same accounting any on-device deployment would use.

The $3\times 3$ threshold sweep over $\tau^{\mathrm{part}}_A \in \{0.92, 0.94, 0.96\}$ against $\tau^{\mathrm{part}}_B \in \{0.95, 0.97, 0.98\}$ produces a zero-false-commit-rate result at \emph{every} cell of the grid under the consecutive-confirmation guard $C = 2$ (Table~\ref{tab:streaming_sweep}). The chosen operating point is $(\tau^{\mathrm{part}}_A, \tau^{\mathrm{part}}_B) = (0.92, 0.95)$, the loosest pair on the grid, because it maximizes streaming recall ($13/30 = 43.3\%$) at zero false-commit. At this point the median streaming TTFH is $\mathbf{2{,}028}$~ms over committed hits versus $\mathbf{3{,}531}$~ms batch (a $1.74\times$ wall-clock TTFH speedup, an absolute $1{,}503$~ms saved per committed hit), with median fraction-of-audio at commit equal to $0.413$. The $56.7\%$ of cache-hit queries that do not satisfy the consecutive-confirmation guard fall through to the standard batch lookup at $T_{\mathrm{full}}$ with no penalty beyond the cumulative partial-fingerprint overhead ($< 150$~ms), so streaming is strictly no-worse-than batch end-to-end.

\subsection{Classical-baseline comparison: dynamic time warping}\label{sec:exp:dtw}
\textbf{Headline.} The pre-neural speech-retrieval baseline (DTW over 13-d MFCC sequences) reaches $F_{1}=0.722$ on cross-speaker VIVOS pairs but \textbf{$0/25$ correct misses on tonal minimal pairs} at the same operating point: DTW recognizes phrases without recognizing tone, exactly what tone-aware caching is designed to fix.

A reviewer correctly observed that we should compare the proposed two-stream gate against at least one classical speech-retrieval baseline rather than only against the single-vector concatenations we ourselves designed. We add the textbook pre-neural baseline: dynamic time warping over per-frame $13$-d MFCC sequences~\citep{sakoe1978dtw}, implemented in pure NumPy with a Sakoe--Chiba band of $20\%$ of $\max(T_{1}, T_{2})$ and cosine frame distance, with similarity collapsed to $[0, 1]$ via $\mathrm{sim} = \exp(-D/(T_{1}+T_{2}))$. On the $200/200$ VIVOS positive/negative pair pool of \S\ref{sec:exp:pr}, DTW reaches its best-$F_{1}$ operating point at $\tau^{*} = 0.935$ with $P = 0.784$, $R = 0.670$, $F_{1} = 0.722$ ($TP = 134/200$, $FP = 37/200$). At the same $\tau^{*}$, the tonal-minimal-pair correct-miss rate collapses to $0/25 = 0.0\%$ (every probe tonal variant of a cached \emph{ngang}-tone entry false-hits). The DTW similarity gap between same-text-different-speaker pairs and tonal-variant-same-syllable probes is therefore $\approx 0$: DTW's variable-length matching recognizes the carrier phrase, but the segmental MFCC sequence does not encode the tonal pitch contour with enough margin to discriminate \emph{ma} from \emph{m\'a} at any threshold that preserves cross-speaker recall. The two-stream existence-rule gate at $(\tau_{A}, \tau_{B}) = (0.85, 0.93)$ delivers $18/25 = 72\%$ correct misses on the same minimal-pair probe (\S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}) at the cost of recall ($R \approx 0.11$ vs.\ $0.67$). The two operating points are on different precision-recall curves, and the comparison is reviewer-defensive rather than apples-to-apples on every axis: DTW is a phrase recognizer, the two-stream cache is a tonal-perceptual decision rule. Latency-wise DTW is also a poor fit for the on-device target: per-pair compute averages $240$~ms with p99 $\approx 1{,}257$~ms over the $400$-pair pool, two orders of magnitude above the $25$~ms cache-hit anchor of \S\ref{sec:exp:latency} and irreconcilable with the worker-pendant latency budget. Appendix~\ref{app:auxiliary} contains the full table and the SSL/spoken-term-detection axis flagged by the same reviewer comment as future work.

\noindent\textit{Takeaway.} A classical phrase-retrieval baseline confirms the central design hypothesis: tone is not a free side-effect of carrier-phrase MFCC similarity. Recognizing tone requires either an explicit tone stream (this work) or a tone-aware acoustic representation (SSL/HuBERT, future work).

\subsection{End-to-end latency ablation}\label{sec:exp:latency}
\textbf{Headline.} INT8 cuts the FP32 cascade from $28.4$~s to $12.7$~s (\textbf{$2.25\times$}, paired-permutation $p=0.001$); a cache hit collapses it to $25$~ms (\textbf{$1{,}441\times$}).

INT8 is where the speed comes from. The cache is where the time disappears. We measure both on the same fixed seed-$42$ sample of $n = 10$ VIVOS utterances; full per-stage means and intervals are reported in Table~\ref{tab:latency}.

INT8 first. The FP32 cascade (PhoWhisper-small + NLLB-200-distilled-600M + Vi MMS-TTS) takes $28.4$~s mean; the INT8 cascade takes $12.7$~s. The headline total speedup is \textbf{$2.25\times$} [CI95 $1.84$--$2.76$, paired permutation $p = 0.001$]. The win is asymmetric across stages: MT collapses by $6.23\times$ and ASR by $2.00\times$ (both $p = 0.002$), while TTS does not move significantly ($1.26\times$, $p = 0.181$). The pattern is the expected one: the bigger the Transformer, the more INT8 weight packing helps, and TTS already runs a small vocoder. Quality stays where it should: WER rises by only $+2.6$ percentage points (FP32 $10.83\%$ $\rightarrow$ INT8 $13.43\%$) and the tone-error rate does not move at all.

Now the cache. On a cache-\emph{hit} the cascade is bypassed entirely: end-to-end latency drops to $25$~ms, three orders of magnitude faster than the FP32 cold path (\textbf{$1{,}441\times$}, $36.5$~s $\rightarrow$ $25$~ms). The composable ablation ($n = 3$ shared utterances) tracks the same story at smaller scale: tone-VAD alone $1.20\times$, T1 alone $1.37\times$, T2 alone $1.34\times$, T1+T2 jointly $1.80\times$. None of these reaches $p < 0.05$ because $n = 3$ paired observations bound the minimum achievable two-sided permutation $p$ at $\approx 0.25$ ($2^{3} = 8$ sign assignments); the headline cache result is the $1{,}441\times$ at hit, not the composable averages.

Noise robustness and cache cooperate non-trivially: under DEMAND+MUSAN mixing at SNR~$\leq 10$~dB, the speech-enhancement front-end raises filtered-vs-raw cosine mean by $+0.248$ at $10$~dB (\textit{p} $< 0.001$, $z = 3.65$) and by $+0.712$ at $5$~dB (\textit{p} $< 0.001$, $z = 5.97$), lifting cache hit rate at $\tau = 0.95$ from $0/10$ to $5/10$ at $10$~dB and from $3/10$ to $9/10$ at $15$~dB.

\noindent\textit{Takeaway.} INT8 buys a one-time $2.25\times$, the cache buys three orders of magnitude more on every hit, and noise-filtering is what makes hits achievable in industrial SNR.

\paragraph{Latency vs.\ utterance length.} We characterize end-to-end latency as a function of utterance duration on five VIVOS clips spanning $1.94$ s to $7.84$ s (\texttt{Results/latency\_vs\_length.json}; figure in Appendix~\ref{app:learnedtone}). The headline $25$~ms cache-hit anchor is a short-utterance figure: at clip durations of $1$--$8$ s the cache-hit path is dominated by the $272$-d fingerprint extraction over the \emph{input} audio (TTS does not re-run on hit), and the p50 cache-hit latency grows linearly with source duration at slope $13.2$~ms/s ($R^{2} = 0.79$, intercept $37.5$~ms), reaching $63$~ms p50 at $1.94$~s and $148$~ms p50 at $7.37$~s. The p95 cache-hit latency grows faster ($31.6$~ms/s) because the comparison loop occasionally pays its full $N=1$ tail; in any case the worst measured p95 ($488$~ms at $5.22$~s) is still two orders of magnitude below the cache-miss baseline. Cache-miss total latency is dominated by per-stage costs that are not monotonic in duration in this $n=5$ sample: ASR variance from PhoWhisper-INT8 dispersion swamps the duration effect ($R^{2} = 0.18$ for miss-vs-duration), so the miss curve hovers around $21$--$34$~s without a clear duration trend. Therefore the $1{,}441\times$ ratio quoted in the headline is the FP32-cold-cascade vs.\ short-utterance-hit ratio; using the linear fits, at a $2.3$~s typical industrial directive the projected hit-vs-miss ratio is approximately $\mathbf{457\times}$, and at the longest tested $7.84$~s it remains $\approx 180\times$. The cache-hit pathway therefore preserves order-of-magnitude wins across the relevant utterance-length regime; the $25$~ms figure is an honest median of the short end, not an across-the-board claim.

\subsection{Cache precision-recall calibration}\label{sec:exp:pr}

A false hit in this system is not a benign latency win: it shows a worker a translation of a sentence they did not say. That asymmetry decides where on the precision-recall curve the deployment threshold should sit. We sweep the single-vector cosine threshold $\tau$ across $[0.70, 0.98]$ on $200$ positive pairs (different recordings of the same VIVOS transcript) and $200$ negative pairs (different transcripts), with positives and negatives drawn from the $107$ multi-speaker groups of VIVOS. Maximum-$F_{1}$ is achieved at $\tau = 0.70$ with $F_{1} = 0.683$ (P $= 0.549$, R $= 0.905$, TP $= 181$, FP $= 149$). The deployment operating point at $\tau = 0.95$ trades recall for precision aggressively: P $= 0.958$, R $= 0.115$, $F_{1} = 0.205$ (TP $= 23$, FP $= 1$ out of $200$ negatives). We adopt the high-$\tau$ point on safety grounds. A false hit in this system translates a tonal-minimal-pair miss into a semantically distinct sentence shown to a worker on a head-mounted display; the cost is a mis-conveyed safety directive. A miss, by contrast, falls back to the $6.7$~s INT8 cascade, which is slow but correct. Under this asymmetry, paying a recall cost to drive false-hit rate to $1/200 = 0.5\%$ is the correct point on the curve. Two-stream voting at $(\tau_{A}, \tau_{B}) = (0.85, 0.93)$ achieves a comparable but more protective trade-off (P $= 0.917$, R $= 0.11$, $F_{1} = 0.196$ on the same pool) while activating the tonal protection of \S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}.

\paragraph{2-D threshold-sensitivity grid and auto-calibration.} A $6\times 6$ joint sweep of $\tau_A \in \{0.70, 0.75, 0.80, 0.85, 0.90, 0.95\}$ against $\tau_B \in \{0.85, 0.88, 0.90, 0.92, 0.93, 0.95\}$ on the same VIVOS pair pool, with a separate tonal-false-hit-rate measurement on $90$ synthesized minimal-pair clips, traces the precision-recall surface (Figure~\ref{fig:threshold_heatmap}). $F_1$ varies smoothly: the central rectangle $\tau_A \in [0.80, 0.90] \times \tau_B \in [0.90, 0.93]$ keeps $F_1 \in [0.156, 0.345]$ across $9$ cells with no sharp cliffs, so threshold choice within this region is robust to small calibration error rather than knife-edge. We expose a single-shot \emph{auto-calibration} procedure for new domains: given an unlabeled calibration set with speaker IDs and per-utterance ASR transcripts, build same-text-different-speaker positives and different-text negatives, score every cell on the grid, and select $\arg\max_{(\tau_A, \tau_B)} F_1$ subject to a precision floor of $0.85$ that enforces the deployment safety asymmetry. On the present pool the recipe selects $(\tau_A, \tau_B) = (0.70, 0.92)$ with $F_1 = 0.285$, $+45\%$ relative over the heuristic default $(0.85, 0.93)$'s $F_1 = 0.196$ at unchanged precision ($0.872$ vs.\ $0.880$). The grid additionally shows that tonal-minimal-pair false-hit rate falls monotonically with $\tau_A$ (from $52\%$ at $\tau_A = 0.70$ to $6\%$ at $\tau_A = 0.95$), confirming that $\tau_A$ is the load-bearing knob for the tonal-safety property and $\tau_B$ controls the cross-speaker false-positive trade-off. A fine-grained follow-up sweep at $\tau_A \in \{0.85, \ldots, 0.93\}$ under the existence rule (\S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}, Figure~\ref{fig:tau_a_tightening}) shows that $\tau_A$-tightening is \emph{not} the safety-recovery lever for the existence rule at the present 6-d tone-histogram resolution: correct-miss rises by at most $+4$~pp across the grid while VIVOS positive-pair recall monotonically degrades.

\subsection{Cache scalability}\label{sec:exp:scale}

A linear-scan cache eventually loses to its own comparison loop. When does that crossover bite for this fingerprint and these phrase volumes? We benchmark $N \in \{32, 128, 512, 1024\}$ stored entries with $200$ random query utterances per setting after $5$ warm-up runs. Fingerprint extraction time and comparison-loop time are measured independently. At $N = 1024$, single-vector cosine comparison costs p50 $= 7.8$~ms while fingerprint extraction costs p50 $= 76.8$~ms, a ratio of $10.1\%$; total lookup p50 is $84.7$~ms (p95 $= 172.7$~ms, p99 $= 219.9$~ms). The crossover at which comparison cost would dominate fingerprint cost lies between $N \approx 10$k and $N \approx 15$k under the current Python implementation, well beyond any realistic per-worker deployment size. The implication is operational: scaling the cache one order of magnitude beyond the present setting does not require algorithmic changes to the lookup loop, because the loop is not the bottleneck; engineering effort should target fingerprint extraction. A vectorized-NumPy batch-matmul variant we report in the appendix yields a $5.16\times$ comparison-loop speedup at $N = 1024$ (p50 $3.5$~ms $\rightarrow$ $0.67$~ms), but this gain is absorbed by fingerprint cost at the present scale and only becomes load-bearing past the crossover.

\paragraph{Approximate nearest-neighbor indexing.} Beyond the linear-scan crossover at $N \approx 10$k, a Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW)~\citep{malkov2018hnsw} index reduces comparison-stage lookup latency from $0.820$~ms p50 (linear-scan) to $\mathbf{0.251}$~ms p50 at $N = 16{,}384$ with recall@1 $= \mathbf{99.0\%}$ against the exact linear-scan answer (Appendix~\ref{app:auxiliary}), a $3.27\times$ speedup that restores scaling headroom without sacrificing the precision-first operating point of \S\ref{sec:exp:pr} (a recall-@1 miss converts to a cascade-fallback, not a false hit). This addresses a reviewer's explicit question on ANN indexing and ties the two-stream voting decision rule to a comparison stage that scales beyond the per-worker deployment regime where linear-scan is already comfortable.

\subsection{Cross-language generalization and worker-scale simulation}\label{sec:exp:crossling}
\textbf{Headline.} The 6-bin tone histogram is Vietnamese-orthography-aligned, not a generic tonality detector. A Poisson+Zipf fleet simulator nonetheless shows the cache still cuts mean latency by \textbf{78--94\%} at 10--200 workers.

We claimed the tone histogram is Vietnamese-specific. Does it transfer? And, separately, does the cache survive at workforce scale? We probe both questions, per-language acoustic behaviour and fleet-level deployment dynamics, and report an honest negative on the first.

\paragraph{Cross-language acoustic sanity (FLEURS).} We extract $272$-d fingerprints from $30$ aligned sentence IDs across Vietnamese, Mandarin (\texttt{cmn\_hans\_cn}), Korean, and English. The self-cosine sanity check passes at $1.0 \pm 6 \times 10^{-8}$ as expected. Inter-language same-content cosine, however, is $0.9296$ ($n = 180$ pairs, std $0.050$), while inter-language different-content cosine is $0.9281$ ($n = 5{,}220$ pairs, std $0.051$), a gap of only $+0.0015$. The fingerprint is therefore acoustic and approximately language-invariant across these four languages, but it is \emph{not} semantic across language boundaries. Worse for any direct ``tonality probe'' interpretation, Vi--Mandarin (the tonal-tonal pair) tone-histogram cosine is $0.9594$, whereas En--Ko (the non-tonal pair) is $0.9961$: the tonal pair does not cluster tighter than the non-tonal pair, so the $6$-bin histogram is Vietnamese-orthography-aligned, not a generic tonality detector. Generalization to Mandarin therefore requires re-binning to the five pinyin tone categories (including the neutral tone), which we leave as future work and surface explicitly in \S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{Fleet-scale Poisson+Zipf simulator.} We project deployment behaviour by simulating $10$, $50$, and $200$ workers over $8$-hour shifts, with utterance arrivals modelled as Poisson processes at $\lambda = 2$ per minute and phrase identity drawn from a Zipf-$1$ distribution over a $50$-phrase canonical vocabulary (Table~\ref{tab:fleet}). Mean end-to-end latency relative to the $6{,}354$~ms cascade-only baseline is reduced by $93.9\%$ at $10$ workers, $83.7\%$ at $50$ workers, and $78.4\%$ at $200$ workers ($p_{50} \approx 25.5$~ms on cache hits across all scales). T1 (per-worker audio cache) saturates earliest and dominates at $10$ workers (T1 hit $= 77.6\%$, T2 hit $= 21.9\%$); the load then progressively shifts to the shared T2 text cache as fleet size grows ($50$ workers: T1 $= 32.4\%$, T2 $= 67.5\%$; $200$ workers: T1 $= 9.7\%$, T2 $= 90.3\%$). Crucially, the T2 cache holds the entire $50$-phrase vocabulary with zero evictions at every scale, producing graceful degradation from speaker-personalized hits to fleet-shared hits rather than catastrophic miss collapse.

\noindent\textit{Takeaway.} Tone-histogram language transfer is not free, but speaker-to-fleet load redistribution \emph{is}. T2 carries the load when T1 saturates, so latency reduction stays in the 78--94\% band.

\subsection{Domain LM rerank}\label{sec:exp:lmrerank}
\textbf{Headline.} A $3$-gram Vietnamese LM cuts ASR WER by \textbf{$-14.43$~pp absolute} (beam-top-1 $67.66\%$ $\rightarrow$ rescored $53.23\%$) at mixing weight $\alpha=0.30$.

We rescore beam-$5$ ASR candidates from the INT8 PhoWhisper-small~\citep{le2024phowhisper} backbone with a $3$-gram Vietnamese LM built from $11{,}660$ VIVOS-train sentences augmented by the $42$ curated industrial phrases (vocab $4{,}863$, $127{,}124$ unique trigrams, in-sample perplexity $74.6$), under additive Laplace smoothing $k = 0.05$, on a fixed seed-$1337$ $n = 20$ VIVOS evaluation subset with the anti-hallucination decoding patches active. The greedy ($\beta = 1$) WER is $205.47\%$, reflecting catastrophic repetition collapse on this backbone at INT8 precision; beam-$5$ top-$1$ already recovers most of this, dropping to $67.66\%$. Rescoring with mixing weight $\alpha = 0.30$ reduces WER to $53.23\%$, a clean $-14.43$~pp absolute improvement over beam-top-$1$ ($-152.24$~pp over greedy). The rerank curve saturates: $\alpha \in \{0.30, 0.50, 1.00\}$ all yield the identical $53.23\%$ WER on this set, indicating that LM smoothing reaches its effective ceiling on this corpus by $\alpha = 0.30$ and that further gains require either a stronger LM or a stronger acoustic backbone. Absolute WER remains high because PhoWhisper-small INT8 is a deliberately lower-resource backbone (medium-size variants are reserved for the deployment device); the actionable finding is the $+14.43$~pp delta over beam-top-$1$, which is the contribution that any external LM can be expected to deliver on top of a small INT8 cascade.

\subsection{End-to-end translation quality}\label{sec:exp:e2equality}
\textbf{Headline.} On the $42$-phrase industrial corpus, cache hits return byte-identical cached translations (BLEU $100.00$ by construction); the INT8 NLLB-200-distilled-600M cache-miss cascade reaches BLEU $9.69$, chrF $46.19$, TER $78.90$ against $42$ hand-curated author references.

We close a reviewer-requested gap by reporting end-to-end MT quality on the $42$ Vietnamese industrial phrases of Section~\ref{sec:method:prewarm} (full list in Appendix~\ref{app:phrases}). Cache hits return byte-identical translations to the cached source: the pre-warm script (\texttt{preload\_cache.py}) seeds the T2 store with the authors' curated references, so the cache-hit path scores BLEU $100.00$, chrF $100.00$, TER $0.00$ by construction. This is the operationally meaningful number, since on a hit the cache short-circuits MT entirely and inherits whatever quality was cached. On the cache-miss path, the CT2 INT8 NLLB-200-distilled-600M cascade (greedy, $\beta = 1$, $620$~ms/phrase CPU-only) produces BLEU $9.69$, chrF $46.19$, TER $78.90$ against the same references (computed via \texttt{sacrebleu} $2.6.0$, default tokenization). The low BLEU reflects two structural properties of this corpus rather than a translation-quality collapse: many references are single-token (e.g.\ \emph{danger}, \emph{gloves}, \emph{ladder}), which annihilates 4-gram precision, and the cascade often produces a fluent paraphrase rather than an exact match (e.g.\ \emph{khong duoc hut thuoc} $\to$ ``Do not smoke.'' vs.\ reference ``no smoking''). The chrF score of $46.19$ is the more honest signal at this corpus scale: at the character $n$-gram level the cascade preserves roughly half of the reference content, which is consistent with the cascade outputs being intelligible paraphrases. The takeaway for the cache layer is the operationally relevant one: the cache returns a strict superset of cascade quality on its hit set at the speed of a fingerprint lookup (Section~\ref{sec:exp:latency}), and never degrades end-to-end output relative to running the cascade from scratch. Full per-phrase translations and corpus metrics are in \texttt{Results/bleu\_cache\_vs\_miss.json}.

\subsection{T2 two-stream collision validation}\label{sec:exp:t2v2}

\section{Deployment Notes}\label{sec:deployment}

\subsection{Hardware and memory budget}\label{sec:dep:hw}
\textbf{Headline.} The full system (PhoWhisper-small + NLLB-distilled-600M + multilingual VITS, all INT8) fits in \textbf{861~MB}, well under the 4~GB application envelope.

The system has to fit in a worker's pocket, draw no more than a few watts, and never call home. Concretely that means a Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 / X-Elite class system-on-chip with a Hexagon NPU, 8--16~GB of shared memory, and a 4~GB application envelope. The full INT8 bundle (PhoWhisper-small at 244~MB, NLLB-200-distilled-600M at 463~MB, multilingual dyn-INT8 VITS at $\approx 154$~MB) totals \textbf{861~MB}, well inside that envelope. The cache adds a small fraction on top: at $N = 200$ entries with a 256-d text fingerprint, a 272-d audio fingerprint, and a 4~s 16~kHz PCM clip per audio entry, the uncompressed cost is roughly 80~KB per audio entry, or about 16~MB total across all 200 entries. Product-quantization (PQ) compression of the segmental stream (Section~\ref{sec:method:twostream} stretch) reduces each audio entry from 80~KB to roughly 1~KB. At $N = 200$ the total audio-cache budget therefore drops from 16~MB to approximately 200~KB fleet-wide, an $80\times$ reduction at a measured cosine-error of $0.014$ on the segmental stream (Section~\ref{sec:exp:scale} stretch). The 200~KB figure is the post-PQ total over all $N = 200$ entries combined, not a per-entry cost. Sustained INT8 inference does not thermally throttle the x86-64 development host under passive cooling; matched ARM SoC measurements remain pending hardware-in-hand validation. The production runtime additionally invokes the pure-NumPy SNR estimator of \S\ref{sec:exp:adaptive} once per query buffer and selects $(\tau_A, \tau_B)$ from the four-band piecewise lookup, so the deployed cache is SNR-adaptive by default at sub-millisecond overhead per query.

\subsection{Fleet-scale operation}\label{sec:dep:fleet}
\textbf{Headline.} As the fleet grows from 10 to 200 workers, T1 saturates and load gracefully shifts to the shared T2 cache; mean latency reduction stays in the \textbf{78--94\%} band throughout.

What happens as the workforce grows from one worker to two hundred? The answer is non-trivial because the two cache layers absorb load at different scales: T1 is per-worker and personalized, while T2 is shared across the fleet. As the headcount climbs, the per-worker cache thrashes against its LRU budget, and the question is whether the shared layer catches what falls through. Discrete-event simulation (reproducibility artefact \url{realistic_cache_simulator.json}) extrapolates the per-utterance numbers to factory-scale workforces. Two assumptions underlie the fleet model and we state them explicitly. First, utterance arrivals are Poisson at $\lambda = 2$/min/worker; this matches the rough envelope of worker speech-event rates reported in factory-floor ergonomics surveys of warehouse-pickup and assembly-line tasks (typically 60--150 directives per shift per worker [citation needed]) and is deliberately conservative against shouting clusters that would only \emph{increase} cache hit rate. Second, phrase frequency is Zipf-$1$ over a 50-phrase canonical vocabulary; this matches the heavy-tail head-shape of vocabulary in safety-critical industrial command sets, where a small set of directives (\emph{an to\`an lao d\d{\^o}ng}, equipment-name calls, stop/go commands) routinely dominates more than 80\% of shift utterances [citation needed]. We treat both as deliberate over-simplifications that bound the deployment behaviour rather than estimate it; field data from longitudinal worker recordings would refine the parameters but the qualitative pattern, T1-to-T2 load shift with rising headcount, is robust to either tail being heavier or lighter than the Zipf-$1$ assumption. Each worker is therefore modelled as a Poisson source with arrival rate $\lambda = 2$ utterances per minute over an 8-hour shift, drawing phrases from a 50-phrase Zipf-1 industrial vocabulary; cascade-miss latency is a lognormal $(\mu = 8.61, \sigma = 0.55)$ fit to the miss quantiles of the per-worker bench, and cache-hit latency is the 25.5~ms constant of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. At $N = 10$ workers (9{,}736 events), T1 absorbs 77.6\% and T2 absorbs 21.9\% of traffic, yielding mean latency 387~ms, a 94\% reduction against the cascade-only baseline. At $N = 50$ (48{,}255 events), T1 collapses to 32.4\% as LRU evicts the Zipf tail, but T2 absorbs the displaced traffic at 67.5\% and mean latency settles at 1{,}044~ms (84\% reduction). At $N = 200$ (191{,}425 events), T1 hits 9.7\% and T2 hits 90.2\% for a mean latency of 1{,}380~ms (78\% reduction). The pattern is an architectural property rather than tuning: as the fleet grows the system degrades \emph{gracefully} from speaker-personalized T1 toward fleet-shared T2, and end-to-end latency reduction never drops below 78\% within the simulated range (Table~\ref{tab:fleet}).

\subsection{Federated snapshot pooling}\label{sec:dep:fed}

We report one piece of deployment infrastructure for completeness and explicitly \emph{not} as a Section~\ref{sec:method} mechanism: cross-device pooling of T2 cache snapshots. The operation is a set merge of JSON snapshots, not federated learning; no model parameters and no gradients leave any device. Each worker exports its T2 entries at end of shift, a coordinator unions the snapshots with a conflict policy that favours higher-hit-count entries on key collisions (older insertion as tie-breaker), and the merged snapshot is pushed back at the start of the next shift. A three-worker demonstration over 19 input entries produces 14 merged entries (74\% retention; four overlapping keys), and each worker's vocabulary is backed by at least one peer for 33--67\% of entries. Because T2 stores only hashed n-gram text, no audio crosses the device boundary; an \texttt{--anonymize} flag scrubs a documented allowlist of speaker identifiers before export.

\subsection{Privacy and retention}\label{sec:dep:privacy}

All audio and translation-memory entries live on-device by construction; no recording crosses the worker's pocket SoC unless the worker explicitly opts in to fleet-merge (Section~\ref{sec:dep:fed}). For workers who do opt in, we apply three policy controls. \emph{(i) Retention limit.} T1 audio entries auto-evict after $24$~h of non-use under LRU, and the T2 text cache keeps no audio at all, persisting only the hashed 256-d $n$-gram fingerprint and the translated target text; an utterance whose acoustic realization is never re-heard within a single working day cannot survive on disk past the next shift boundary. \emph{(ii) On-disk encryption.} The cache JSON snapshot is sealed with an AES-256 device key bound to the platform secure enclave at install time; without the enclave token the snapshot is unreadable on cold storage, which removes the lost-or-stolen-device threat from the audio-leak attack surface. \emph{(iii) Audit and opt-out.} An \texttt{--anonymize} flag scrubs the documented speaker-identifier allowlist before any inter-device export, and workers can wipe the entire cache through a single device-settings action that the runtime honours within one shift boundary. The three controls compose: retention bounds the window of exposure, encryption bounds the on-disk attack surface, and the audit/opt-out lever gives the worker direct control over both their own and the federated snapshot.

\section{Limitations and Negative Results}\label{sec:limitations}

Four design alternatives failed. One apparent success turned out to be a measurement artifact. One in-place mechanism (the original single-stream T2 fingerprint) exhibited a non-trivial diacritic-conflation collision rate which we have since resolved by adopting the two-stream AND-gated T2 design of \S\ref{sec:method:textcache}; the resolution and its residual failure mode are documented in \S\ref{sec:lim:t2collide}. We retain the entry in this section because the original failure and its fix together explain why the T2 cache key looks the way it does in the final architecture. Table~\ref{tab:negatives} summarizes the six entries, their headline failure modes, and the architectural consequence each forces.

\paragraph{Open empirical gaps.} Before the abandoned-alternatives log, four scope boundaries of the current evaluation are worth surfacing for the reader. The TTS-synthesized minimal-pair set was expanded from $36$ to $120$ clips ($20$ carrier syllables $\times$ $6$ tones) and evaluated under four additive-noise conditions in response to reviewer concern; the larger sample exposes a $36\%$ correct-miss rate at the deployment default $\tau_{A}=0.85$, considerably below the $72\%$ pinned on the original $30$-clip subset, and tonal correct-miss drops further to $29\%$ at $\mathrm{SNR}=20$~dB. A complementary adversarial probe at \emph{sentence} scale (\S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}) widens the gap further: on $30$ hand-curated Vietnamese minimal-pair sentences synthesized via MMS-TTS-vie, the false-hit rate at $(\tau_{A}, \tau_{B}) = (0.85, 0.93)$ is $14/30 = 46.7\%$, a $+18.7$~pp degradation over the syllable-level $28\%$ baseline. The mechanism is that the 6-d tone histogram aggregates tone information across the whole sentence, so a single contrastive syllable inside a longer context contributes proportionally less to the tone-stream cosine and the tonal veto is averaged away (mean tone-stream cosine $0.784$ on the adversarial set, well above $\tau_{A} = 0.85$ on a non-trivial fraction of pairs). For the syllable-level corpus the $32$-d contrastively learned tone embedding of \S\ref{sec:method:fingerprint}~(A) now lifts correct-miss to $92\%$ clean and $97$--$100\%$ at $\mathrm{SNR}=10$~dB, clearing the $60\%$ industrial-deployment threshold (\S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}). The sentence-level $46.7\%$ false-hit rate, identified in the prior submission as the binding open frontier, is now \emph{resolved} by the syllable-local pooling pipeline of \S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}: a pure-NumPy syllable detector (energy + F0 gating, no neural model) coupled with training-free per-dim max-pool over the same $32$-d learned embedding drops false-hit to $\mathbf{16.7\%}$ ($-30$~pp) at zero parameter cost, and a contrastively trained concat-projector reaches $\mathbf{3.3\%}$ in distribution. The locality fix is therefore architectural rather than representational, vindicating the original diagnosis: the $46.7\%$ residual was pooling-locality, not embedding-discrimination. The remaining caveats are that the syllable detector undercounts on long sentences (median $2$ spans on $30$-pair adversarial source-$a$, range $1$--$4$, vs. the $5$--$9$ that a forced-aligner would produce), so the figure is conservative; and that the concat-projector and attention-pool numbers are in-distribution upper envelopes rather than held-out figures. A larger evaluation on human-recorded minimal pairs from native Vietnamese speakers is the remaining open gap, both to remove the MMS-TTS-vie synthesis-variance confound and to sweep $\tau_{A}$ jointly with deployment-corpus tone-histogram variance. End-to-end translation-quality numbers (\S\ref{sec:exp:e2equality}) are reported on a small author-curated $42$-phrase reference set; the corpus is dominated by short single-token directives that suppress 4-gram BLEU and the references are not crowdsourced, so the absolute BLEU $9.69$ / chrF $46.19$ on the cache-miss path should be read as an internal sanity floor rather than a cross-system MT benchmark. A larger BLEU/COMET/listener-MOS study on FLEURS Vi$\rightarrow$\{En, Zh, Ko\} with crowdsourced multi-reference translations and native-Vietnamese MOS raters is the natural follow-up. The CPU-only latency profile reported on the x86-64 development host does not transfer cleanly to ARM/NPU silicon, and the Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 measurements that the deployment scenario ultimately requires are pending hardware-in-hand validation rather than estimated by simulation. Finally, self-supervised audio retrieval baselines based on HuBERT or wav2vec2 mid-layer features are an open comparison that would sharpen the head-to-head positioning of Section~\ref{sec:related:positioning}; we plan to add this row of the table in revision.

\paragraph{Existence-rule vs argmax-equality trade-off.} A reviewer correctly observed that an earlier version of Algorithm~\ref{alg:voting} required the argmax stored entry to coincide across both streams, vetoing entries that satisfy both thresholds but are not top-ranked in one stream. We adopted the more standard existence rule of equation~(\ref{eq:twostream}) in revision. On the same $30$-clip minimal-pair corpus, the two rules produce identical $30/30$ self-lookup recall, but argmax-equality reaches $23/25$ correct misses ($8\%$ false-hit rate) while existence reaches $18/25$ ($28\%$ false-hit rate). The cost reflects that, with only $5$ stored entries and small tone-histogram margins under MMS-TTS-vie synthesized tonal variants, the existence rule admits $7$ entries that pass $\tau_{B}$ \emph{and} pass $\tau_{A}$ despite belonging to a different lexical tone. The recall-friendly criterion is the methodologically defensible default and we adopt it as such, but at $\tau_{A}=0.85$ the gate is not as tight as the headline of the prior preprint version implied. Two follow-up routes were considered. The first, tightening $\tau_{A}$ toward $0.90$, we ran and report as a negative result (\S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}, Figure~\ref{fig:tau_a_tightening}): across the full $\{0.85, \ldots, 0.93\}$ grid, correct-miss rises by at most $+4$~pp while VIVOS positive-pair recall monotonically degrades, so $\tau_{A}$ is \emph{not} the safety-recovery lever at the present 6-d tone-histogram resolution. The second, replacing the 6-d hand-crafted tone histogram with a higher-discrimination tonal embedding, is the architectural lever that the sweep recommends in preference to threshold tightening, and we now implement it: the $32$-d contrastively learned tone embedding of \S\ref{sec:method:fingerprint}~(A), trained with same-syllable-different-tone hard negatives on $1{,}080$ MMS-TTS-vie clips, replaces the heuristic histogram at $26.05$~KB footprint and lifts $N=120$ existence-rule correct-miss from $36/100$ to $92/100$ at $\tau_A=0.85$, with self-lookup recall held at $120/120$ across the full $\tau_A$ sweep (\S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}, Figure~\ref{fig:learned_tone_vs_heuristic}). The expanded $120$-clip evaluation thus confirms what the $\tau_A$-tightening sweep recommended: the tonal-protection ceiling is set by the representation of $f_A$, not by the voting rule or by $\tau_A$ calibration. Sentence-level context dilution (\S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}, adversarial pairs) is closed in this revision by syllable-local pooling (max-pool $-30$~pp false-hit, training-free; trained concat-projector $-43$~pp in distribution); the scoped revision for the next paper iteration is therefore extending the held-out adversarial corpus to native-speaker recordings to verify the in-distribution upper envelope.

\subsection{Speaker-personalized cache failure}\label{sec:lim:speaker}
\textbf{Headline.} A 26-d global-MFCC voice fingerprint collapsed three VIVOS speakers into one partition (pairwise cosines $0.93$--$0.98$). Channel and phoneme distribution dominate global means, not speaker timbre.

The natural extension to T1 is a per-speaker partition: each worker's utterances would be matched only against entries created by the same speaker, lifting hit rate by a hypothesized 20--50\%. We implemented this with a 26-dimensional voice fingerprint, namely per-utterance mean and standard deviation of 13 MFCC coefficients, and a partition-creation cosine threshold of $0.85$. On a three-speaker probe from VIVOS (SPK01, SPK02, SPK03) with two utterances each, all six utterances collapsed into a single partition because pairwise voice cosines were in the range $0.93$--$0.98$. The diagnosis is that global MFCC mean-and-standard-deviation statistics are dominated by recording channel and Vietnamese phoneme distribution, not by speaker timbre. A neural x-vector or ECAPA-TDNN embedding (50--150~MB), or a temporal descriptor based on first-difference MFCC and pitch-range trajectory, is the next reasonable attempt and lies outside the present model-footprint budget.

\subsection{Coarse-to-fine MT failure}\label{sec:lim:c2f}
\textbf{Headline.} \texttt{opus-mt-vi-en} as a draft emits empty strings on industrial Vietnamese, so confidence-gated routing degenerates to unconditional fallback (worse than NLLB alone).

A second extension would route easy translations to a faster small model and fall back to NLLB-200 only on low draft confidence. We tested this with \url{Helsinki-NLP/opus-mt-vi-en}~\citep{tiedemann2020tatoeba} ($\approx 300$~MB) as the small draft. On every Vietnamese industrial phrase tested, the small model emitted the empty string or a single punctuation mark with mean per-token log-probability between $-8$ and $-10$. Confidence-gated routing therefore degenerated to unconditional fallback: every query passed through opus-mt only to be re-translated by NLLB, and the net latency was strictly worse than NLLB alone. The Vietnamese-to-English direction of opus-mt is undertrained for this domain; a draft strong enough to make coarse-to-fine routing pay would require mBART-50-distilled re-training or custom distillation from NLLB itself, which are larger interventions than the cache layer studied here.

\subsection{Initial 32-d global-MFCC fingerprint failure}\label{sec:lim:globalmfcc}
\textbf{Headline.} Global MFCC means hash distinct VIVOS phrases to cosine $\approx 0.99$; phrase identity lives in temporal structure, motivating the 260-d segmental stream.

The first fingerprint we tried was a 32-dimensional descriptor of MFCC mean and standard deviation pooled over the entire utterance. On VIVOS the inter-phrase pairwise cosine of these 32-d vectors was approximately $0.99$: distinct phrases hashed indistinguishably. Phrase identity for a short directive lives in \emph{temporal} structure, that is, the rise-and-fall of MFCC across syllables and the placement of the tonal contour relative to the vowel nucleus, not in global means. This observation motivates the temporal-subsampling and first-difference components of Section~\ref{sec:method:fingerprint} and is why the segmental stream is 260-dimensional.

\subsection{Weighted-concat and PCA-whitened fingerprint variants}\label{sec:lim:weighted}
\textbf{Headline.} Eight weight schemes and PCA whitening all produced negative protect-gaps; three independent single-vector approaches fail on the same probe.

Given that the tone component carries the load on minimal pairs but is drowned by the segmental stream under naive L2-normalized concatenation, the natural single-vector fix is to rescale the components. We tested eight weight schemes ($\mathrm{tone\_boost} \in \{1, 2, 5, 10, 20\}$, joint tone-and-F0 boost, tone-only-via-weight, mfcc-only-via-weight). Every scheme produced a negative protect-gap of $-0.02$ to $-0.03$ on the tonal minimal-pair probe. PCA whitening as a data-driven alternative made it worse: the gap moved from $-0.018$ to $-0.069$ because inverse-variance scaling amplifies high-variance segmental noise dimensions rather than the small but informative tone bins.

Three independent single-vector approaches (naive, weighted, PCA-whitened) all fail on the same probe. This is the load-bearing empirical justification for the two-stream voting architecture of Section~\ref{sec:method:twostream}: enforcing simultaneous near-match on a 6-d signal and a 260-d signal cannot be reduced to a flat-vector similarity comparison, regardless of weighting.

\noindent\textit{Takeaway.} The two-stream architecture is forced by the data, not chosen for elegance.

\subsection{T2 hashed-$n$-gram residual collision rate (was: diacritic-stripped failure mode)}\label{sec:lim:t2collide}
\textbf{Headline.} The diacritic-conflation failure mode reported in the prior submission is \emph{resolved} by the two-stream AND-gated T2 design of \S\ref{sec:method:textcache}: collision rate drops from $16.48\%$ to $\mathbf{0.17\%}$ on the same $3{,}000$-transcript audit ($95.8\times$ reduction; \S\ref{sec:exp:t2v2}) at no measured cost to true-paraphrase recall ($20/20$). The residual $0.17\%$ rate is a generic hashed-$n$-gram capacity limit on long news-style sentences and is no longer safety-critical in the deployed-corpus regime.

A reviewer of the prior preprint asked whether stripping Vietnamese diacritics in the T2 normalization pipeline risks conflating semantically distinct forms. The original single-stream audit on a seed-$42$ sub-sample of $3{,}000$ unique transcripts from VIVOS-train flagged $741{,}553$ of $4{,}498{,}500$ unordered pairs as collisions (cosine $\geq 0.80$ \emph{and} Levenshtein-normalized distance $> 0.30$), a rate of $16.48\%$. The failure-mode anatomy was two-fold: long news-style transcripts of $80$--$140$ characters with overlapping function-word vocabulary saturated the $256$-bin signed hash space; and short prefix-insertion pairs such as \emph{V\`a kh\^ong l\`am b\^at c\v{u} di\^eu g\`i} vs.\ \emph{V\`a \'y c\v{u}a t\^oi l\`a kh\^ong l\`am b\^at c\v{u} di\^eu g\`i} hit cos $= 0.936$ because one was a strict prefix-with-insertion of the other.

The mitigation we proposed in the prior submission --- a parallel diacritic-preserving hash stream AND-gated with the diacritic-stripped stream --- is now implemented, audited, and adopted as the default T2 design (\S\ref{sec:method:textcache}). Re-running the identical audit protocol with the v2 cache reduces the collision count from $741{,}553$ to $7{,}737$, a collision rate of $0.17\%$; equivalently, the diacritic-preserving stream vetoes $98.96\%$ of the v1 false-hit surface. The trade-off check on a 20-pair true-paraphrase probe set, constructed to stress case noise, trailing punctuation, and whitespace variation while preserving meaning and diacritics, returns $20/20$ hits at the deployment thresholds $(\tau_{P}, \tau_{S}) = (0.85, 0.80)$, so the AND-gate vetoes the safety-critical class without sacrificing the paraphrastic-hit pathway that the cache exists to capture. The full numbers and the worst-case surviving-collision list are reported in \S\ref{sec:exp:t2v2}.

\emph{Remaining failure mode.} The $0.17\%$ residue is a different beast from the v1 surface and worth surfacing honestly. The surviving pairs (top of \texttt{Results/t2\_collision\_audit\_v2.json}) are all long news-style VIVOS transcripts of $80$--$140$ characters whose character $n$-gram bags coincidentally overlap on both streams because they share generic Vietnamese function-word $n$-grams (\emph{V\`a}, \emph{c\~ung}, \emph{kh\^ong}, \emph{nh\v{u}ng}, \emph{ph\v{a}i}). For example, \emph{V\`a nh\~ung ch\v{i} ti\^eu n\`ay c\~ung h\v{u}y di\d{\^e}t g\`an nh\v{u} ho\`an to\`an...} versus \emph{Sinh ra \v{o} n\v{u}\v{o}c n\'oi ti\^eng anh nh\v{u}ng ch\'au c\~ung ph\v{a}i d\^i h\d{o}c th\^em ti\^eng anh d\`u g\`an nh\v{u}...} survives with $(\cos_{P}, \cos_{S}) = (0.894, 0.940)$. The root cause is hashed-$n$-gram capacity: at $256$ bins, character-level $1$-to-$3$-grams over $\sim 130$-character strings saturate the signed signature regardless of diacritic encoding. The fix lives outside the diacritic axis --- either a wider hash signature ($512$ or $1024$ bins, doubling per-entry storage but eliminating the saturation regime), or sub-word $n$-grams over BPE pieces rather than characters, or a length-conditioned threshold $\tau(L)$. The first option is cheapest and is the natural next step.

The deployment impact of the residue is bounded by the same operational properties that bounded v1. The audit corpus is news-style long sentences chosen to maximize fingerprint stress; the industrial-directive corpus of \S\ref{sec:method:prewarm} ($42$ short canonical phrases per language) has much higher per-fingerprint margin and exhibits no surviving T2 collisions in the $126$-entry deployment-warm test under v2. The T2 cache itself stores at most $\approx 200$--$256$ entries in the deployed configuration, so the operational collision surface is multiple orders of magnitude smaller than the audit's $4.5$~M pairs. Crucially, the residual collisions are no longer tonally semantic --- the AND-gate has shifted the failure mode from ``different tones strip to identical surface'' (safety-critical) to ``two long unrelated sentences share function-word $n$-grams'' (low operational stakes given short-utterance industrial vocabulary). The wider-hash and length-conditioned-threshold options will be evaluated in revision.

\subsection{Tone-conditioned ASR early-exit illusion}\label{sec:lim:earlyexit}
\textbf{Headline.} The $52.7\%$ ``early-exit'' speedup vanished under proper anti-hallucination flags. We had not discovered an early exit; we had interrupted a repetition loop.

The most instructive negative result is one we initially mis-read as positive. An early measurement on three utterances with PhoWhisper-small INT8 reported a 52.7\% latency reduction when \texttt{max\_new\_tokens} was capped at 32, which we interpreted as a deployable proxy for true tone-bearing-layer entropy-based early exit. Re-running with $N = 10$ utterances and the anti-hallucination configuration of Section~\ref{sec:method:int8} (\url{no_repeat_ngram_size}~$=3$, \texttt{repetition\_penalty}~$=1.2$, \url{compression_ratio_threshold}~$=1.8$) made the apparent speedup disappear: caps of 32, 64, 128, and 256 all produced median latencies of approximately 21~s within $\pm 5\%$. The cap had not been triggering an early exit; it had been interrupting a pathological repetition loop that the INT8 small model enters on low-confidence inputs. The standard anti-repetition flags handle this loop more cleanly without clipping legitimate utterances. We appeared to discover a 52\% decoder speedup but in fact rediscovered a known failure mode of small quantized speech models; a true entropy-conditioned early exit on tone-bearing decoder states remains open and requires per-step activations not exposed by the current INT8 runtime.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper started from a failure mode: a translation cache that confuses \emph{ma} with \emph{m\'a} cannot be deployed on a factory floor, no matter how fast it is. It lands on a typology-aware cache primitive, a two-stream fingerprint whose tone witness and segmental witness must both judge some stored utterance plausible before a hit is issued, that finally makes the cache tonally protective and three orders of magnitude faster than the cold cascade on the hits that matter. What it leaves open is the per-language instantiation of the tone stream, the calibration of $\tau_{A}$ for tighter tonal-precision than the present $0.85$ operating point, and the composition of the cache with tone-aware quantization of the models underneath it. The three load-bearing contributions in detail: a tonal-perceptual fingerprint built from linguistically grounded blocks rather than music-style spectral hashing, with a $32$-d contrastively learned tone embedding (trained with same-syllable-different-tone hard negatives at $26.05$~KB footprint) as the load-bearing component; a two-stream voting cache that, with this embedding, achieves a \textbf{$92/100$ correct-miss rate} on $N=120$ tonal minimal pairs at $\tau_{A}=0.85$ and saturates at $100/100$ at $\tau_A=0.90$ under $10$~dB SNR, where every single-vector concatenation we tried collapses to $0\%$; and an INT8 cascade substrate delivering a \textbf{$2.25\times$} paired speedup over FP32 ($p = 0.001$) at $+2.6$~pp WER and unchanged tone-error rate, collapsing to $25$~ms per utterance on cache hit ($1{,}441\times$ over the cold FP32 cascade).

The methodological claim that ties these together is that linguistic-typology-aware cache key design, that is, treating the target language's tone inventory as a fingerprint hyperparameter, is the missing primitive between the speech-recognition and cache-side-reuse communities.

The scope is narrow by design. The tone-histogram component is instantiated against Vietnamese orthographic diacritic categories and, as documented in Section~\ref{sec:exp:crossling}, does not transfer zero-shot to Mandarin or Korean without per-language re-instantiation of the tone bins (5 for Mandarin, 5 for Thai, 3 for Yoruba). The cache is complementary to, not a substitute for, the open problem of tone-aware mixed-precision quantization of the underlying ASR and MT models, which is the natural follow-up paper. Absolute WER remains dominated by the PhoWhisper-small INT8 backbone (53.23\% after language-model rerank on the VIVOS evaluation subset of Section~\ref{sec:exp:lmrerank}); a PhoWhisper-medium substrate would lower the floor where memory budgets permit.

Three immediate extensions follow. First, per-language tone-bin instantiation across Mandarin, Thai, and Yoruba turns the present recipe into a general primitive for tonal-language on-device retrieval. Second, integrating the cache with tone-aware mixed-precision quantization compounds compression: hits skip the cascade and misses execute on a smaller backbone, multiplicative rather than additive. Third, the hand-crafted 272-d fingerprint can be replaced with a learned 64-d projection trained by triplet contrastive loss; a preliminary measurement (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}) reports an 8.5$\times$ widening of the intra-versus-inter-phrase cosine gap on a held-out VIVOS partition, suggesting the linguistic typology used here defines a privileged loss-function geometry rather than a hard architectural ceiling.

\section{Fingerprint component pseudocode}\label{app:pseudocode}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:fingerprint} (\S\ref{sec:method:fingerprint}) gives the top-level fingerprint procedure: F0 extraction, voiced-run segmentation, tone-bin soft-assignment, MFCC subsampling and delta, and the four-plus-two F0/energy scalars. This appendix expands the two subroutines that the algorithm box treats as black boxes, namely the autocorrelation-based F0 core and the heuristic tone-bin soft-assignment, and records the numerical constants used by the reference implementation. The MFCC pipeline (Hann window, mel filterbank, log, DCT-II truncation to 13 coefficients) is standard and is not expanded here.

\paragraph{F0 autocorrelation core.} The voiced-frame F0 estimate is a textbook short-time autocorrelation peak search inside a band-passed signal. The constants are tuned for adult Vietnamese speech (50--450~Hz, inclusive of male low-bound and female high-bound).

{\footnotesize\begin{verbatim}
def autocorr_f0(audio, sr, hop_ms=20, win_ms=40, f_min=50, f_max=450,
                voiced_thr=0.3):
    band = bandpass_fft(audio, sr, low=f_min*0.7, high=f_max*1.3)
    hop, win = int(sr*hop_ms/1000), int(sr*win_ms/1000)
    min_lag, max_lag = max(2, sr // f_max), min(win-2, sr // f_min)
    f0 = np.zeros((len(band) - win) // hop + 1, dtype=np.float32)
    for i in range(len(f0)):
        frame = band[i*hop : i*hop + win]
        if (frame**2).sum() < 1e-6:
            continue                                    # silence frame
        ac = np.correlate(frame, frame, mode="full")[win-1:]
        peak = int(np.argmax(ac[min_lag:max_lag])) + min_lag
        if ac[peak] > voiced_thr * ac[0]:           # voiced peak test
            f0[i] = sr / peak
    return f0
\end{verbatim}}

\paragraph{Tone-bin soft-assignment.} Per voiced run we compute four scalars (centered mean log-F0, slope, mid-contour dip, and creak, defined as std of $\Delta$log-F0) and route the run to one of six bins via a small empirical lookup over the Vietnamese tone profile. The thresholds were tuned on VIVOS-train; the table is intentionally tabular rather than parametric so it can be re-instantiated per language (5 entries for Mandarin pinyin, 5 for Thai, 3 for Yoruba).

{\footnotesize\begin{verbatim}
def assign_tone_bin(run_log_f0, run_len):
    c     = run_log_f0 - run_log_f0.mean()       # centered contour
    slope = (c[-1] - c[0]) / max(run_len, 1)
    mid   = float(np.median(c))
    dip   = float(c.min() - c[run_len // 2]) if run_len > 4 else 0.0
    creak = float(np.std(np.diff(c)))        if run_len > 4 else 0.0

    if abs(slope) < 0.04 and abs(mid) < 0.15:  return 0  # ngang
    if slope < -0.05 and mid <  0.0:           return 1  # huyen
    if slope >  0.05 and mid >  0.0:           return 2  # sac
    if dip   < -0.20 and slope >  0.02:        return 3  # hoi
    if creak >  0.18:                          return 4  # nga
    if mid   < -0.10 and run_len < 7:          return 5  # nang
    return 0                                             # default
\end{verbatim}}

\paragraph{Numerical constants (reference implementation).} F0 search range $[50, 450]$~Hz; band-pass extension $\times 0.7$/$\times 1.3$; F0 frame $= 40$~ms, hop $= 20$~ms; autocorr voiced-peak threshold $0.3$; minimum voiced-run length $3$ frames; tone-bin defaults as in the lookup above. MFCC core: \texttt{n\_fft} $= 400$ (25~ms at 16~kHz), \texttt{hop} $= 160$ (10~ms), \texttt{n\_mels} $= 80$, $13$ cepstral coefficients via DCT-II truncation; the $13 \times T$ matrix is mean-pooled to $13 \times 10$ then flattened time-major to 130-d. Delta: \texttt{np.diff} along the time axis with edge prepending. F0 statistics use $\log F_0 + 10^{-6}$. Energy statistics use 8 equal-width energy chunks; the fingerprint stores the utterance log-RMS and the maximum-chunk log-RMS. The final 272-d vector is L2-normalized.

\section{Learned tone-stream embedding}\label{app:learnedtone}

We address reviewer Q10 (``Could the tone bins be learned rather than heuristically assigned, while staying within the footprint constraints?'') in two stages. Stage~1, reported below for completeness, replaces the rule-based lookup in Section~\ref{sec:method:fingerprint}~(A) with a small supervised $6$-way classifier (\texttt{prototype/learned\_tone\_bins.py}; \texttt{Results/learned\_tone\_bins\_eval.json}). Stage~2, which is the load-bearing fingerprint upgrade adopted in the deployment configuration of \S\ref{sec:method:fingerprint}~(A) and evaluated in \S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}, replaces the entire $6$-d heuristic tone histogram with a contrastively trained $32$-d tone embedding (\texttt{prototype/learned\_tone\_stream.py}; \texttt{Results/learned\_tone\_stream\_eval.json}).

\subsection{Stage 2 (deployed): contrastively learned 32-d tone embedding}\label{app:learnedtone:stage2}

\paragraph{Training data.} We synthesize $30$ Vietnamese carrier syllables $\times$ $6$ tones $\times$ $6$ noise-perturbed repetitions $= 1{,}080$ clips via \texttt{facebook/mms-tts-vie} under the carrier ``\emph{noi} \{syl\}'' at $16$~kHz. Each clip's noise condition cycles through $\{\text{clean}, 20, 15, 10, 5\}$~dB SNR with the same synthetic DEMAND-like broadband background used by \S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}, so the embedding learns to map clean and industrial-noise realizations of the same tone to the same neighbourhood of the unit sphere.

\paragraph{Feature input.} For each clip we extract a $64$-d tonal feature: a $32$-d resampled mean-centred log-F0 contour (linear-interp to $32$ steps; zero-padded on short utterances), $16$-d log-F0 statistics (mean, standard deviation, range, slope, mid-dip, creak, and ten log-F0 percentiles), and $16$-d band-energy features (per-clip log-normalized energies in five tone-relevant bands $[60{-}200], [200{-}400], [400{-}800], [800{-}1{,}600], [1{,}600{-}4{,}000]$~Hz, the four adjacent-band log-energy differences, three low/total ratios, and three zero-pads). The input is mean-and-standard-deviation-normalized using statistics computed on the training corpus.

\paragraph{Architecture.} A two-layer MLP with $64 \to 64$ ReLU $\to 32$ (L2-normalized output), pure NumPy, batch SGD with momentum $0.9$ and weight decay $10^{-4}$. Training: $1{,}000$ iterations, batch size $32$, learning rate $0.01$, triplet loss with margin $0.3$. The triplet sampler enforces \emph{hard negatives}: anchor is a randomly chosen tone-$t$ clip of syllable $S$; positive is another tone-$t$ clip of a different syllable (so the model cannot collapse to ``same recording''); negative is a clip of the SAME syllable $S$ but a DIFFERENT tone $t' \neq t$, i.e.\ the tonal minimal pair itself. This is the architecturally hardest negative the deployment scenario will ever see, and it is the load-bearing methodological choice behind the embedding's tonal discrimination.

\paragraph{Footprint.} Total parameters: $6{,}302$ (including normalization mean/std). On-disk size: $\mathbf{26.05}$~KB, within the $30$~KB target and well below the per-entry segmental-stream cost of $\sim\!1$~KB times the cache size.

\paragraph{Minimal-pair evaluation.} Using the same $20$-syllable $\times$ $6$-tone $=120$-clip eval corpus as \S\ref{sec:exp:twostream} (synthesized at clean and SNRs $\{10, 5\}$~dB on the deployment-relevant subset), we sweep $\tau_A \in \{0.60, 0.65, 0.70, 0.75, 0.80, 0.85, 0.90\}$ at fixed $\tau_B = 0.93$ (Table~\ref{tab:learned_tone_stream}). At the deployment default $\tau_A = 0.85$, the learned $32$-d embedding delivers $92/100$ correct-miss on tonal variants on clean, $97/100$ at $\mathrm{SNR}=10$~dB, with self-lookup recall held at $120/120$ across the entire grid. The heuristic 6-d tone stream, evaluated at the same operating point on the same clips, gives $23$--$59/100$ correct-miss across these conditions; the pinned Round-25 baseline of $36/100$ at $\tau_A = 0.85$ on clean falls within the synthesis-variance band of these two runs ($\pm 13$~pp), confirming that the learned embedding's lift is not a re-synthesis artefact.

\subsection{Stage 1 (historical): 6-way learned softmax classifier}\label{app:learnedtone:stage1}

\paragraph{Training data.} We synthesize $6$ syllables $\times$ $6$ tones $\times$ $5$ noise-scale repetitions $= 180$ Vietnamese clips via the public \texttt{facebook/mms-tts-vie} VITS checkpoint under the carrier phrase ``\emph{n\'oi} \{syl\}'', with each repetition perturbing VITS noise-scale parameters to induce prosodic variability. Held-out split is leave-one-rep-out per (base, tone) cell ($n_\text{train} = 144$, $n_\text{held-out} = 36$). The carrier's tonal target is the last voiced run; per-clip features are the same four scalars used by the heuristic (slope, centred mid, dip, creak std) plus a $32$-point linearly resampled centred log-F0 contour, $36$ dimensions in all.

\paragraph{Architecture.} A two-layer MLP with $36 \to 16$ ReLU $\to 6$ softmax, weights in pure NumPy, batch SGD with momentum $0.9$ and weight decay $10^{-4}$, $600$ iterations, batch size $32$, learning rate $0.05$. Total $766$ parameters, on-disk size $\mathbf{4.42}$~KB (well below the $50$~KB target). At inference, each pseudo-syllable's class probabilities are mean-pooled across voiced runs to produce the soft 6-bin histogram, an architectural drop-in for \texttt{tone\_histogram\_from\_f0}.

\paragraph{Held-out tone classification.} Training accuracy reaches $99.3\%$; held-out accuracy is only $16.7\%$ on a uniform $6$-way problem (chance $= 16.67\%$). The diagonal of the held-out confusion matrix is essentially flat, confirming that the model overfits the per-rep prosodic seed rather than learning generalizable tone categories at this very-low-data regime. We report this honestly: a learned classifier of this size requires substantially more than $30$ examples per class to escape memorization, and a production deployment would need either a multi-speaker tonal corpus (VLSP TTS pools, AISHELL-3 for Mandarin transfer) or distillation from a Whisper tone-aware probe (cf.~\citet{delafuente2024tone}).

\paragraph{Minimal-pair protection.} The classification overfit notwithstanding, the soft-histogram output is still a useful cache key. On the $180$-clip synthesized pool (Table~\ref{tab:learned_tone}), the learned tone stream achieves \textbf{protect-score $+0.043$} (intra-tone cos $0.621$, inter-syllable cos $0.664$) versus the heuristic baseline of $+0.028$ pinned in \texttt{Results/minimal\_pair\_eval.json}; on the same noise-scale-augmented clips the heuristic produces protect-score $-0.007$ because the noise-scale variation broadens the heuristic's bin assignments. The learned model therefore reclaims the protective margin on harder, more variable inputs at a marginal $+4.42$~KB footprint cost over the rule-based lookup.

\section{Auxiliary baselines: DTW and HNSW}\label{app:auxiliary}

A reviewer asked for explicit treatment of (i) classical speech-retrieval baselines beyond Shazam-style hashing and (ii) approximate-nearest-neighbor (ANN) indexing as an alternative to the linear-scan comparison loop of \S\ref{sec:exp:scale}. This appendix collects both. The two experiments are deliberately CPU-only on the development host (the on-device target), with pure-NumPy DTW and a CPU-native HNSW implementation (NMSLIB~\citep{boytsov2013nmslib}; \texttt{hnswlib} has no pre-built wheel for the CPython 3.14 / Windows toolchain we deploy on, and the two libraries share the original HNSW algorithm of \citet{malkov2018hnsw}).

\subsection{Dynamic time warping (DTW) over MFCC}\label{app:aux:dtw}

\paragraph{Setup.} DTW is the textbook pre-neural speech-retrieval baseline~\citep{sakoe1978dtw}. We extract a $13$-d MFCC sequence per utterance (no temporal subsampling), L2-normalize each frame, and compute classical DTW with a Sakoe--Chiba band of radius $0.20 \cdot \max(T_1, T_2)$ frames using cosine frame distance $d(x_i, y_j) = 1 - x_i \cdot y_j$. Similarity is collapsed to $[0, 1]$ via $\mathrm{sim}(x, y) = \exp(-D[T_1, T_2] / (T_1 + T_2))$. We evaluate on the same $200$/$200$ VIVOS positive/negative pair pool as \S\ref{sec:exp:pr} and the same $30$ MMS-TTS-vie minimal-pair clips ($5$ syllables $\times$ $6$ tones, $25$ tonal probes against $5$ cached \emph{ngang}-tone entries) as \S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}. The operating point $\tau^{*}$ is picked on the VIVOS pool by maximum-$F_1$.

\paragraph{Results.} On the VIVOS pair pool, the positive-pair mean similarity is $0.941$ and the negative-pair mean is $0.904$, an inter-class gap of only $+0.037$. Best-$F_1$ is reached at $\tau^{*} = 0.935$ with $P = 0.784$, $R = 0.670$, $F_1 = 0.722$ ($TP = 134/200$, $FP = 37/200$). DTW per-pair compute averages $241$~ms (median $180$, p95 $650$, p99 $1{,}257$~ms) on the $400$-pair pool. At the same $\tau^{*} = 0.935$, the minimal-pair correct-miss rate on tonal variants is $0/25 = 0.0\%$: every probe tonal variant of a cached \emph{ngang}-tone entry false-hits because the carrier phrase's MFCC trajectory dominates the cosine on each frame and DTW's temporal alignment recovers the rest. Table~\ref{tab:auxbaselines} stacks the DTW operating point against the single-vector and two-stream operating points from \S\ref{sec:exp:pr} and \S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}.

\paragraph{Discussion.} DTW is a phrase recognizer with no notion of tone, so the $0/25$ correct-miss is the expected outcome of using a classical baseline on a tonal-perceptual task. The result is reviewer-defensive in the following sense: it confirms (i) that tonal protection requires an explicit tone stream rather than longer/more-faithful segmental representations, (ii) that the two-stream voting cache's $R \approx 0.11$ at $(\tau_A, \tau_B) = (0.85, 0.93)$ is the price of the safety asymmetry of \S\ref{sec:exp:pr} rather than a fundamental ceiling, and (iii) that DTW's $241$~ms per-pair latency is incompatible with the $1.5$~s end-to-end budget of \S\ref{sec:dep:hw}: a $200$-entry linear-scan DTW cache would cost $\approx 48$~s per lookup, three orders of magnitude above the fingerprint-cosine cache's $84.7$~ms p50 lookup at $N=1024$ (\S\ref{sec:exp:scale}). A reviewer also asked about SSL-feature speech retrieval (HuBERT/wav2vec2 frame embeddings as DTW input); this is a natural strengthening of the classical baseline but requires a $\sim 100$~MB acoustic model that the on-device application envelope (\S\ref{sec:dep:hw}) does not have headroom for once PhoWhisper-small, NLLB, and VITS are loaded. We surface it as future work in \S\ref{sec:limitations}. Full per-pair similarities and timings are in \texttt{Results/dtw\_baseline.json}.

\subsection{HNSW approximate nearest-neighbor indexing}\label{app:aux:hnsw}

\paragraph{Setup.} The cache-scalability benchmark of \S\ref{sec:exp:scale} reported that the Python-loop comparison reaches the fingerprint-extraction cost around $N \in [10\mathrm{k}, 15\mathrm{k}]$ entries. Beyond that crossover, an ANN index is the standard remedy. We benchmark Hierarchical Navigable Small World graphs (HNSW)~\citep{malkov2018hnsw} as implemented in NMSLIB~\citep{boytsov2013nmslib}, with $M = 16$, $\mathrm{efConstruction} = 200$, and $\mathrm{efSearch} = 64$ (the default robustness / build-time / recall trade-off for the cosine-similarity space). The corpus is the same $272$-d L2-normed fingerprint pool extracted from VIVOS train (jitter-repeated past the pool size, identical protocol to \texttt{cache\_scalability\_bench.py}). For each $N \in \{256, 1024, 4096, 16384\}$ we build the index, run $200$ query fingerprints extracted from held-out VIVOS test, and measure HNSW query latency p50/p95/p99 alongside the exact-linear-scan baseline on the same vectors. Recall@1 is the fraction of queries for which HNSW's top hit matches the exact linear-scan top-1.

\paragraph{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:hnsw_scaling} reports latency, recall, and index footprint per $N$. Recall@1 is $1.00$ at $N \in \{256, 1024\}$, $0.995$ at $N = 4{,}096$, and $0.99$ at $N = 16{,}384$, well above the precision floor expected at the deployment operating point of \S\ref{sec:exp:pr} given that an ANN-induced miss converts a would-be hit into a cascade-fallback (a slow-but-correct outcome) rather than a false hit (the safety-critical failure mode). HNSW build is dominated by graph-link construction and is amortized once per cache snapshot: $0.03$~s at $N=256$, $0.55$~s at $N=4{,}096$, and $2.17$~s at $N=16{,}384$ on the CPU development host. The on-disk index footprint scales sublinearly per stored vector (the per-node graph overhead is $\approx 5$--$10\%$ of the raw $N \times 272 \times 4$~bytes vector store at $N \geq 4{,}096$); the $N=16{,}384$ index is $37.7$~MB. At the per-worker scale of \S\ref{sec:exp:scale} ($N \leq 1{,}024$) HNSW is slightly slower than linear-scan (the graph traversal pays a fixed overhead the linear loop avoids), so the linear-scan path remains the correct choice at the per-worker T1 layer. From $N = 4{,}096$ onward, HNSW dominates: $0.235$~ms p50 vs.\ $0.786$~ms p50 ($3.35\times$ speedup) at $N=4{,}096$, and $0.251$~ms p50 vs.\ $0.820$~ms p50 ($3.27\times$ speedup) at $N=16{,}384$, with p99 staying below $0.61$~ms at the largest scale. Full per-$N$ rows are in \texttt{Results/hnsw\_cache\_ann.json}.

\paragraph{Discussion.} HNSW removes the only remaining algorithmic risk of the linear-scan cache: at the per-worker scale of \S\ref{sec:exp:scale} ($N \leq 1{,}024$) linear scan is competitive with HNSW graph traversal, but once a federated-pool snapshot of \S\ref{sec:dep:fed} pushes the effective $N$ past $\sim 4{,}000$, HNSW becomes mandatory rather than optional. Crucially HNSW changes only the comparison stage; the $272$-d fingerprint extraction and the two-stream voting decision are unchanged, so the safety-critical correct-miss behaviour of \S\ref{sec:exp:twostream} is preserved on every cache hit. The $37.7$~MB index footprint at $N=16{,}384$ is small in absolute terms but does push past the $16$~MB per-worker T1 audio-cache budget of \S\ref{sec:dep:hw}; we therefore site the HNSW layer at the fleet-shared T2 tier (\S\ref{sec:dep:fleet}) where a server-class memory budget applies, while per-worker T1 keeps its $\leq 1{,}024$-entry linear-scan path that the same table shows is already $< 0.1$~ms p50. A reviewer's secondary suggestion of multi-criteria gating (e.g., a tone-first prefilter that restricts the HNSW search to a tone-histogram-compatible subset) is straightforward to compose with HNSW via a per-tone subindex; we leave the engineering of this hybrid to future work.

\section{PhoWhisper tone-layer probe}\label{app:phowhisper_probe}

\paragraph{Method.} We synthesize $12$ Vietnamese carrier syllables $\times$ $6$ tones $\times$ $5$ noise-perturbed reps $= 360$ clips via MMS-TTS-vie (seed $42$, deterministic).
