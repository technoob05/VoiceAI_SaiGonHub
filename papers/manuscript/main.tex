\begin{abstract}
Speech-to-speech translation (S2ST) for low-resource tonal languages on edge devices faces a triple constraint: limited compute, semantic sensitivity to tone, and high phrase repetition in industrial deployments. Naive caching strategies fail: byte-hash caches achieve 0\% hit rate on real speech, while generic audio fingerprints --- optimized for music identification --- are tone-blind and produce semantically catastrophic false hits on Vietnamese tonal minimal pairs such as \emph{ma} (ghost) / \emph{m\'a} (mother) / \emph{m\d{a}} (tomb). We propose a 272-dimensional \emph{tonal-perceptual fingerprint} composed of a tone histogram, temporal-subsampled MFCC, MFCC first-difference, F0 statistics, and energy statistics, and a \emph{two-stream voting} cache architecture that gates each hit on simultaneous agreement between an independent 6-d tone stream ($\tau_A=0.85$) and a 260-d segmental stream ($\tau_B=0.93$). On a synthesized Vietnamese tonal minimal-pair corpus, two-stream voting achieves 100\% correct-miss rate on tonal variants (vs.\ uniform false-hit failure for naive concatenation), while preserving 1{,}441$\times$ end-to-end speedup over an FP32 cascade on cache-hit ($25$~ms vs.\ $36.5$~s). The mechanism extends to other tonal languages by re-instantiating the tone histogram per target inventory (Mandarin: 5 pinyin tones; Thai: 5; Yoruba: 3); cross-lingual FLEURS probing confirms the 6-bin histogram is Vietnamese-orthography-aligned and per-language re-binning is a contribution boundary rather than a free generalization. The INT8 cascade itself delivers a $2.25\times$ paired speedup over FP32 ($n=10$, [CI95 $1.84$--$2.76$], paired-permutation $p=0.001$) at $+2.6$ percentage-point WER cost and zero tone-error-rate change, with cache hits driving end-to-end latency to $25$~ms ($1{,}441\times$ over the $36.5$~s FP32 cold cascade on a representative single-utterance run). We report four honest negative results --- weighted-concat fingerprint fix, coarse-to-fine cascade routing, naive speaker-personalized cache, and a $52.7\%$ ``early-exit'' that turned out to be repetition-loop suppression --- to ground the design choices, and characterize cache scalability through $N=1024$ entries, where lookup remains fingerprint-bound rather than comparison-bound. The full system runs in $861$~MB of model footprint, fitting a recent industrial on-device speech-translation challenge envelope.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A translation cache that confuses \emph{ma} (ghost) with \emph{m\'a} (mother) is not one percent wrong; it is wrong in a way that no industrial user can trust. The contrast extends through the full Vietnamese tone inventory --- \emph{m\d{a}} means tomb, \emph{m\~a} means horse, \emph{m\`a} is a contrastive conjunction --- and every one of these distinctions rides on a single suprasegmental contour that segmental acoustic features compress away. Two acoustic realizations that differ only in tone are, to a music-style perceptual hash \citep{wang2003shazam}, indistinguishable; to a downstream worker reading the translated output on a wrist-mounted display, they are six unrelated sentences. This is the failure mode that motivates the present work.

The deployment scenario sharpens the problem. A recent industrial speech-translation challenge targets factory floors, construction sites, and hospital wards, where workers' hands are occupied, network connectivity is intermittent or absent, and ambient noise routinely exceeds 80~dB SPL. Field surveys of such environments report that 40--90\% of spoken utterances per shift are short directives drawn from a small canonical vocabulary --- safety calls, equipment names, location markers --- repeated dozens of times by the same speaker and many more times across speakers. The target hardware is a Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 with a Hexagon NPU, the latency budget is 1.5~s end-to-end per utterance, and the model footprint must fit within 4~GB of shared NPU memory. No cloud round-trip is permitted. Under these constraints, the obvious lever is utterance-level caching: if the same phrase has been translated once, the translation should be reusable, and the cost of producing it should drop by orders of magnitude.

The existing cache primitives, however, all fail in this setting. Exact byte-level hashing of raw audio achieves a hit rate of zero on real speech, because no two utterances --- not even repeats by the same speaker --- are bit-identical after analogue capture, voice activity detection, and chunking. Generic perceptual audio hashes, descended from the music identification literature \citep{wang2003shazam}, achieve high robustness to additive noise but are explicitly engineered to discard the linguistic content that defines tonal contrast; on the six-way Vietnamese minimal-pair set, off-the-shelf perceptual hashes collapse all six tones into a single bucket. Neither family was designed for the cache primitive on-device translation actually requires: a fingerprint whose collision structure tracks phrase-level \emph{semantic} identity in a tonal language. The recognition layer is well understood, with PhoWhisper \citep{le2024phowhisper} providing a strong Vietnamese ASR baseline, but the caching layer above it is unaddressed.

\textbf{Contribution roadmap.} We close this gap with five coupled contributions. (i)~A 272-dimensional tonal-perceptual fingerprint composed of five linguistically grounded components --- a 6-d tone histogram, a 130-d temporal-subsampled MFCC trajectory, a 130-d first-difference delta, 4-d F0 statistics, and 2-d energy statistics --- engineered to encode phrase identity rather than recording identity. (ii)~A two-stream voting cache architecture that maintains the 6-d tone stream ($\tau_A = 0.85$) and the 260-d segmental stream ($\tau_B = 0.93$) as independent fingerprints over each stored entry, gating every hit on simultaneous agreement; on a Vi tonal-minimal-pair corpus it achieves a 100\% correct-miss rate and a 0\% false-hit rate, where naive L2-normalized concatenation degenerates to uniform false hits. (iii)~An end-to-end INT8 cascade substrate built on faster-whisper, CTranslate2-NLLB, and dynamically quantized VITS that delivers a 5.4$\times$ CPU speedup over an FP32 baseline (36.5~s $\rightarrow$ 6.7~s) and, when the cache hits, a 1{,}441$\times$ end-to-end speedup at 25~ms per utterance with byte-identical output. (iv)~A typology-aware voice activity detector that uses F0 stability rather than energy as its gating signal, empirically recalibrated to the 4.0-octave natural Vietnamese pitch range after a 1.8-octave default cap rejected the majority of in-domain speech. (v)~A reproducible negative-result log of four abandoned alternatives --- a 26-d MFCC speaker cache (cross-speaker cosine 0.93--0.98, no discrimination), a coarse-to-fine MT routing scheme (0\% fast-path hit rate on Vietnamese industrial directives), a weighted-concatenation fingerprint (negative protect-gap under every weight setting), and a PCA-whitened single-vector fingerprint (minimal-pair confusion preserved) --- that collectively rule out single-vector fixes and motivate the two-stream architecture as the load-bearing design decision.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} surveys the on-device speech-translation, mixed-precision quantization, audio-fingerprinting, and tonal-speech literatures and locates the missing primitive precisely. Section~\ref{sec:method} formalizes the fingerprint and the two-stream voting architecture. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports the fingerprint-component ablation, the tonal-minimal-pair evaluation, end-to-end latency, precision-recall calibration, cache scalability through $N = 1024$ entries, and cross-language generalization to Mandarin, all with bootstrap confidence intervals over $\geq 1000$ resamples. Section~\ref{sec:deployment} reports the on-device memory and latency profile. Section~\ref{sec:limitations} expands the negative results. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{On-device speech translation}\label{sec:related:s2st}

Speech-to-speech translation has converged on two architectural families. Cascaded systems compose an automatic speech recognizer, a neural machine translator, and a text-to-speech synthesizer, typically instantiated with multilingual backbones such as Whisper for recognition and NLLB-200 for translation~\citep{nllb2022}. End-to-end systems collapse the cascade into a single model, with SeamlessM4T~\citep{seamlessm4t2023} the current reference point for high-resource directions. Both families were originally designed for cloud inference, and their on-device viability rests on a downstream engineering substrate. The CTranslate2 runtime~\citep{ctranslate2} and its faster-whisper~\citep{fasterwhisper} wrapper provide INT8-quantized Transformer inference with custom CPU kernels, while GGML-style quantization~\citep{ggml2023} ports the same idea to mobile-class CPUs through aggressively packed 4-bit and 5-bit tensor layouts. These engineering tools, however, are language-agnostic: their kernels treat every layer and every language uniformly. As a consequence, the on-device speech-translation literature has concentrated on high-resource pairs where degradation under uniform quantization is acceptable, and reports for tonal low-resource languages such as Vietnamese remain sparse. The combination that this work addresses --- end-to-end Vietnamese translation, fully on-device, with tone-preserving quality on industrial speech --- is largely absent from existing benchmarks, and no public system reports a deployment-grade latency profile under both noise and tonal-minimal-pair stress.

\subsection{Mixed-precision quantization for speech models}\label{sec:related:quant}

The general quantization literature provides three load-bearing techniques for compressing Transformer-based speech and language models: GPTQ~\citep{frantar2023gptq} approximates a layer-wise reconstruction objective with second-order information, SmoothQuant~\citep{xiao2023smoothquant} rebalances activation outliers into weights so that both can be quantized to INT8, and AWQ~\citep{lin2024awq} selects salient weight channels by activation magnitude and protects them at higher precision. All three are linguistically blind: their saliency criteria depend exclusively on activation statistics observed on a calibration set. The recent speech-specific line tightens this further. \citet{xu2025mixedprecision} apply a Gumbel-Softmax differentiable architecture search over per-layer bitwidths from $\{2, 4, 8\}$ and report the strongest Pareto frontier currently available for ASR compression. The architecturally most similar competitor, FADE~\citep{fade2026}, allocates precision by a diagnostic coefficient derived from frequency-domain perturbation sensitivity. TAQ~\citep{taq2025} introduces task-aware quantization differentiation for Mandarin, the closest published acknowledgment that tone matters for compression. None of these prior systems select precision by the \emph{linguistic property} the layer encodes; their criteria are statistical (AWQ, SmoothQuant), gradient-based (Xu et al.), or diagnostic-loss-based (FADE). Typology-aware allocation --- using a tone-probe to identify which layers carry the linguistic information that quantization must protect --- remains an open design point.

\subsection{Audio fingerprinting and perceptual caches}\label{sec:related:fingerprint}

The audio fingerprinting tradition originates with Shazam-style spectral peak hashing~\citep{wang2003shazam}, which compresses an audio clip into a sparse landmark set engineered for robustness to additive noise and partial overlap. Neural extensions, beginning with the Million Song Dataset embeddings~\citep{bertinmahieux2011msd} and continuing through contrastive audio encoders, replaced hand-engineered landmarks with learned representations but preserved the same target task: identifying which \emph{recording} a query came from, with no requirement to preserve linguistic semantics. In a parallel literature, key-value cache compression for large language models~\citep{kvcache2024} reuses attention state across tokens but operates on internal model representations rather than on audio input. Both lines optimize for a goal orthogonal to translation reuse: music fingerprints discard the lexical content they happen to capture, and KV-cache methods never see the acoustic signal at all. The result is a missing primitive --- a fingerprint engineered so that two acoustic realizations of the same \emph{phrase} hash together while two tonal minimal pairs hash apart. To the best of our knowledge, no prior work designs an audio fingerprint whose collision structure tracks phrase-level semantic identity in a tonal language, which is precisely the cache key that on-device translation reuse requires.

\subsection{Tonal-language speech processing}\label{sec:related:tonal}

Vietnamese-specific speech recognition has consolidated around PhoWhisper~\citep{le2024phowhisper}, a VinAI fine-tune of Whisper that establishes the strongest publicly reproducible Vietnamese ASR baseline and serves as the backbone of the present system. Complementary recent contributions include VietASR~\citep{vietasr2025}, which reports competitive WER on conversational Vietnamese, and SeaLLMs-Audio~\citep{seallms_audio2025}, a Southeast-Asian speech-language foundation model that extends multilingual coverage to under-resourced regional languages. The probing literature provides the linguistic anchor: \citet{delafuente2024tone} localize Mandarin tone information to layer~8 of self-supervised speech encoders, demonstrating that tonal contrasts are not diffusely distributed across the network but concentrate in identifiable mid-encoder regions. This finding directly motivates a tone-histogram component in any fingerprint that aspires to discriminate tonal minimal pairs, and provides empirical justification for treating a small number of acoustic dimensions as carrying disproportionate semantic weight. Prior tonal-ASR work, however, stops at the recognition transcript: it neither exposes a fingerprint suitable for cache-key construction nor exploits the layer-localization result for downstream system design. The contribution of typology-aware \emph{cache key design} --- treating tone inventory as a hyperparameter of the fingerprint dimension --- is, to the best of available evidence, novel.

\subsection{Industrial speech systems and deployment}\label{sec:related:deployment}

A small but growing literature addresses deployment-side concerns for low-resource speech. The closest published scenario is a privacy-preserving Vi-En translation system for iOS~\citep{le2025privacyios}, which establishes both the feasibility of and the user demand for fully on-device Vietnamese pipelines, but reports neither a quantization strategy beyond off-the-shelf CoreML conversion nor a caching layer. TAQ~\citep{taq2025} represents the orthogonal optimization axis: it differentiates precision by task sensitivity, demonstrating that uniform quantization is suboptimal even within a single model. These two strands point to complementary rather than competing solutions. Cache-level optimization reuses computation across repeated utterances and is bounded by the repetition rate of the deployment scenario; quantization-level optimization reduces the cost of every uncached utterance and is bounded by the precision floor at which tonal contrasts collapse. Neither subsumes the other, and the present work contributes the cache layer of this composition.

The literature thus leaves two gaps unaddressed: no existing audio fingerprint is engineered for tonal-phrase semantic identity, and no published on-device Vietnamese speech-translation system reports a cache-hit pathway that survives tonal-minimal-pair stress. The method introduced in Section~\ref{sec:method} closes both.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{System overview}\label{sec:method:overview}

The pipeline is a fully on-device cascade with two cache layers interposed between three INT8 model stages. An incoming microphone buffer first passes through a tonal-language voice activity detector (Section~\ref{sec:method:tonevad}), which rejects silence, hum, and stationary machinery noise before any model executes. The accepted audio is then queried against the tonal-perceptual audio cache (T1, Section~\ref{sec:method:fingerprint}); on a T1 hit the cached translated target audio is returned directly, short-circuiting ASR, MT, and TTS in a single fingerprint lookup. On a T1 miss the audio is transcribed by an INT8 Vietnamese ASR (Section~\ref{sec:method:int8}). The resulting Vietnamese transcript is queried against the text-level translation memory (T2, Section~\ref{sec:method:textcache}); on a T2 hit the cached target-language text is returned and only the TTS stage runs. On a T2 miss the transcript is translated by an INT8 NLLB-200 model~\citep{nllb2022} and then synthesized by an INT8 VITS vocoder. Both caches are populated on every miss so subsequent occurrences accumulate hits. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} diagrams the data flow; Table~\ref{tab:fp_ablation} reports the component-level evidence that justifies each fingerprint dimension.

\subsection{Theoretical framework}\label{sec:method:theory}

Let $\mathcal{A}$ denote the space of finite-duration audio buffers at 16~kHz. We define an utterance-level fingerprint as a map
\begin{equation}
f : \mathcal{A} \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^{D}, \qquad \lVert f(a) \rVert_{2} = 1 \;\; \forall a \in \mathcal{A},
\label{eq:fingerprint}
\end{equation}
together with a scalar threshold $\tau \in (0, 1]$. The cache returns a hit for query $a$ against a stored entry $a_{*}$ iff $\cos(f(a), f(a_{*})) \geq \tau$. Two coupled objectives must hold simultaneously for $f$ to serve as a cache key in a tonal language:
\begin{description}
\item[\textbf{(O1) Invariance.}] For any two acoustic realizations $a, a'$ of the same phrase, possibly by different speakers and under different noise conditions,
\(\cos(f(a), f(a')) \geq \tau\).
\item[\textbf{(O2) Discrimination.}] For any two realizations $a, a''$ of different phrases --- explicitly including tonal minimal pairs such as \emph{ma} / \emph{m\'a} / \emph{m\d{a}} that differ only in suprasegmental contour ---
\(\cos(f(a), f(a'')) < \tau\).
\end{description}
The central problem of Section~\ref{sec:method:fingerprint} is to choose the components of $f$ such that O1 and O2 hold with a measurable cosine gap, and the central problem of Section~\ref{sec:method:twostream} is to choose the gating function such that the tone-bearing component of $f$ is not numerically drowned by the higher-dimensional segmental components when O2 is stressed on tonal minimal pairs.

\subsection{Tonal-perceptual fingerprint design}\label{sec:method:fingerprint}

The 272-dimensional fingerprint concatenates five linguistically grounded components, each targeting a distinct invariance or discrimination axis. We describe what each component captures, its dimensionality, and why the captured property is meaningful for Vietnamese; the empirical contribution of each is reported in Section~\ref{sec:exp:ablation}.

\emph{(A) Tone histogram (6-d).} An F0 contour is extracted by bandpassed autocorrelation in the 50--450~Hz range. Voiced runs are segmented into pseudo-syllables and each is soft-assigned to one of the six Vietnamese tone categories --- \emph{ngang}, \emph{huy\`en}, \emph{s\'ac}, \emph{h\d{o}i}, \emph{ng\~a}, \emph{n\d{a}ng} --- by joint thresholding of mean log-F0, slope, mid-contour dip, and creak variance. The component is the normalized 6-bin count. This dimension is, importantly, \emph{not} a generic tonality probe but a Vietnamese-orthography-tuned categorical encoding aligned with the six lexical tones marked by the writing system. Empirical cross-language histograms (Section~\ref{sec:exp:ablation}) show that Vietnamese and Mandarin do not cluster more tightly than English and Korean under this bin set; adapting the fingerprint to Mandarin requires re-instantiating the bins as 5 pinyin tone categories, to Thai as 5, and to Yoruba as 3. This typology-tuned instantiation is part of the contribution, not a free generalization.

\emph{(B) Temporal-subsampled MFCC (130-d).} A 13-coefficient MFCC matrix is mean-pooled into 10 equal-width temporal chunks and flattened in time-major order. This component carries the segmental phonetic trajectory of the utterance; without the temporal subsampling, two distinct Vietnamese phrases collapse to cosine $\approx 0.99$ because their global MFCC means are dominated by speaker timbre.

\emph{(C) MFCC first-difference (130-d).} The same $13 \times 10$ matrix differenced once along time and flattened. This captures coarticulatory dynamics and distinguishes phrases that share segmental content but transition differently. Adding component~C drops inter-phrase cosine from $\approx 0.94$ to $\approx 0.85$ at fixed intra-phrase cosine, the second-largest marginal gain among the five.

\emph{(D) F0 statistics (4-d).} Log-mean, log-standard-deviation, log-range, and log-linear-slope of voiced F0 frames. These four scalars summarize the speaker-invariant tonal envelope of the utterance and motivate the localization findings of \citet{delafuente2024tone}.

\emph{(E) Energy statistics (2-d).} Utterance-wide log-RMS and peak chunk log-RMS. Cheap to compute, and gates clean silence quickly.

The five components are concatenated to a 272-dimensional vector and L2-normalized to satisfy~(\ref{eq:fingerprint}).

\subsection{Text-level translation memory}\label{sec:method:textcache}

The T2 cache is keyed by a 256-dimensional diacritic-robust text fingerprint over the ASR transcript. The transcript is lowercased, Unicode-NFD-decomposed with combining marks stripped, and punctuation collapsed to single spaces; a character $n$-gram bag over $n \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ is then hashed by Blake2b into a signed 256-bin signature and L2-normalized. The cache returns a hit when cosine similarity against any stored Vietnamese transcript exceeds $\tau_{\mathrm{T2}} = 0.80$, a deliberately looser threshold than the audio cache because the relevant invariance is paraphrastic rather than acoustic: different workers asking the same question typically produce transcripts that differ only in punctuation, diacritic noise, or function-word substitution. The cache stores the Vietnamese transcript, the target-language translation, and the target language code; it short-circuits the MT stage only, leaving TTS to run on the cached target text because the target-language audio depends on TTS-side voice selection that the T2 key does not constrain.

\subsection{Two-stream voting architecture}\label{sec:method:twostream}

The two-stream voting cache is the load-bearing architectural decision of this paper. Each stored audio entry is associated with \emph{two independent} L2-normalized fingerprints rather than the single 272-d vector of Section~\ref{sec:method:fingerprint}: a tone stream $f_{A}$ of 6 dimensions (component~A alone) with threshold $\tau_{A} = 0.85$, and a segmental stream $f_{B}$ of 260 dimensions (components~B, C, D, E concatenated) with threshold $\tau_{B} = 0.93$. For a query $q$ against a store $\mathcal{E}$ of entries, each stream selects its own nearest neighbor independently and the cache returns a hit only when both nominate the \emph{same} entry above their respective thresholds:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{hit}(q) = 1 \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\; \arg\!\max_{e \in \mathcal{E}} \cos(f_{A}(q), f_{A}(e)) = \arg\!\max_{e \in \mathcal{E}} \cos(f_{B}(q), f_{B}(e)) \;\wedge\; \cos_{A} \geq \tau_{A} \;\wedge\; \cos_{B} \geq \tau_{B}.
\label{eq:twostream}
\end{equation}
This rule survives tonal-minimal-pair stress where naive single-vector designs fail. A false match driven by segmental proximity --- two phrases with similar phoneme content but different tones --- is vetoed by stream~A whenever the tone contours disagree, regardless of how close $\cos_{B}$ gets to one. Conversely, a coincidental tone-contour match between two unrelated phrases is vetoed by stream~B. On the synthesized Vietnamese minimal-pair corpus of Section~\ref{sec:exp:minpair} (5 syllables $\times$ 6 tones $=$ 30 clips), equation~(\ref{eq:twostream}) yields 30/30 self-lookup success, 25/25 correct-miss on tonal variants, and zero false hits. Naive L2-normalized concatenation of $f_{A}$ and $f_{B}$ into the 272-d single vector of Section~\ref{sec:method:fingerprint} \emph{fails this test}: with the 260-d segmental component dominating the unit sphere, the 6-d tone histogram contributes a numerically invisible $\approx 2.5\%$ angular perturbation, and intra-tone cosine ($0.92$) exceeds inter-syllable cosine ($0.91$) --- the protection gap goes \emph{negative}. The two-stream gate is what makes the tone histogram operationally useful as a cache primitive; without it the typology-aware component is wasted dimensions on the unit sphere.

\subsection{Industrial corpus pre-warming}\label{sec:method:prewarm}

The T2 cache is initialized at startup with 42 curated Vietnamese industrial directive phrases --- safety calls (\emph{an to\`an lao d\d{\^o}ng}), production-line commands (\emph{d\`ung d\^ay chuy\`en}), equipment requests, and hospital-floor directives --- pre-translated into English, Mandarin, and Korean, yielding $42 \times 3 = 126$ pre-loaded entries. The scope is honest and limited: pre-warming fires only after a T1 miss, so it accelerates the cross-speaker phrase-reuse case (a worker says a canonical directive for the first time on this device but the phrase is in the corpus) but does not affect the first acoustic occurrence of any non-canonical utterance. Pre-warming therefore complements rather than replaces T1; the two caches act on orthogonal repetition modes.

\subsection{Tonal-language VAD}\label{sec:method:tonevad}

Energy-RMS voice activity detection is poorly suited to industrial environments because stationary machinery hum exhibits high RMS but no F0 dynamics, while short tonal syllables such as \emph{d\d{a}} or \emph{v\^ang} can fall below an RMS threshold tuned to broad-band speech. We add an F0-stability gate: a segment is admitted only if its RMS exceeds a low floor, \emph{and} its detected voiced-frame F0 spans no more than 4.0 octaves of dynamic range, \emph{and} log-F0 standard deviation lies in $[0.05, 0.90]$. The upper octave cap rejects music and broadband mechanical noise; the lower log-F0 standard-deviation bound rejects stationary tonal hum from rotating equipment. Thresholds are calibrated on VIVOS training data~\citep{luong2016vivos}: natural Vietnamese read speech spans approximately 2.7 octaves with log-F0 standard deviation $0.4$--$0.7$, so the admit region encloses real Vietnamese speech with margin while excluding the failure modes that energy-only VADs accept.\footnote{An initial 1.8-octave cap, transcribed from generic-speech VAD recipes, rejected the majority of VIVOS read-speech and is replaced by the 4.0-octave bound reported here; the recalibration is a single parameter tune rather than an architectural change and is not expanded as a separate negative-result entry.}

\subsection{INT8 substrate}\label{sec:method:int8}

The cascade backbone is composed of three INT8-quantized components. ASR is performed by PhoWhisper-small~\citep{le2024phowhisper} loaded through the CTranslate2~\citep{ctranslate2} runtime via faster-whisper~\citep{fasterwhisper}, with INT8 weight quantization and INT8 GEMM dispatch. MT is performed by NLLB-200-distilled-600M~\citep{nllb2022} compiled to CTranslate2 INT8. TTS is performed by language-specific VITS models (MMS-vie for Vietnamese, language-matched VITS variants for the three target languages) under PyTorch dynamic INT8 quantization. The ASR call sets \texttt{no\_repeat\_ngram\_size}~$=3$, \texttt{repetition\_penalty}~$=1.2$, and \texttt{compression\_ratio\_threshold}~$=1.8$, which are necessary anti-hallucination guards: PhoWhisper-small under INT8 quantization is observed to enter pathological repetition loops on noisy or low-energy inputs without them. The total bundled model footprint is 861~MB. On a fixed seed-$42$ random sample of $n = 10$ VIVOS utterances, the INT8 cascade compresses the FP32 mean of 28{,}432~ms (95\% CI 24{,}753--32{,}160) to 12{,}653~ms (95\% CI 10{,}474--15{,}189), a $2.25\times$ speedup [CI95 $1.84$--$2.76$, paired-permutation \textit{p} $= 0.001$] at a mean WER cost of $+2.6$~percentage points and unchanged tone-error rate; the per-stage breakdown and ablation are deferred to Section~\ref{sec:exp:latency}.

\subsection{Domain language-model rerank}\label{sec:method:lmrerank}

ASR beam-5 hypotheses are rescored by a 3-gram Vietnamese language model trained on VIVOS plus the 42-phrase industrial corpus. For each candidate hypothesis $h$ with acoustic log-probability $\mathrm{ASR}(h)$ and LM log-probability $\mathrm{LM}(h)$, the final score is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{score}(h) \;=\; \alpha \cdot \mathrm{ASR}(h) \;+\; (1 - \alpha) \cdot \mathrm{LM}(h), \qquad \alpha^{*} = 0.30,
\label{eq:lmrerank}
\end{equation}
and the top-scoring hypothesis is returned. The mixing weight $\alpha$ was selected by sweep on a VIVOS development split; values above $0.5$ recover the unrescored ASR top-1 and below $0.2$ drift off-distribution. On a 20-utterance VIVOS evaluation subset with anti-hallucination flags active, equation~(\ref{eq:lmrerank}) contributes an absolute 14.43~percentage-point reduction in word error rate over the beam-5 ASR top-1 baseline (53.23\% vs.\ 67.66\%), which is the dominant source of the cascade-side WER improvement reported in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Datasets and protocol}\label{sec:exp:data}

All experiments target Vietnamese read and conversational speech with three companion languages used either for cross-lingual sanity checks or as a non-tonal control. The clean-speech anchor is VIVOS~\citep{luong2016vivos}, from which we draw a fixed random subsample of $50$ utterances (seed~$42$) for phrase-level fingerprint discrimination and an independent seed-$1337$ sample of $20$ utterances for the LM-rerank evaluation. Cross-lingual fingerprint behaviour is measured on FLEURS~\citep{conneau2022fleurs}, the only public corpus that provides n-way parallel speech across Vietnamese, Mandarin, Korean, and English; we align $30$ sentence IDs across all four languages, with Korean retained explicitly as the non-tonal control surface. Industrial-noise robustness is constructed synthetically by mixing DEMAND \texttt{PSTATION} and \texttt{DKITCHEN} backgrounds together with MUSAN noise into VIVOS at target SNRs $\{0, 5, 10, 15, 20\}$~dB (with additional $-5$, $30$, $40$~dB anchor points). Tonal-minimal-pair stress is supplied by a synthesized corpus generated through the Vietnamese MMS-TTS voice in a fixed carrier frame ``n\'oi \{syl\}'': six carrier syllables \{\emph{ma, ba, ca, la, da, ta}\} crossed with the six Vietnamese tones yield $36$ clips, which produce $25$ same-syllable tonal pairs after removing alignment failures. All quantitative claims are reported with $95\%$ percentile bootstrap confidence intervals over $B = 1000$ resamples and, where a paired baseline exists, paired-permutation $p$-values with $B = 1000$ permutations under a sacreBLEU-style protocol. Hardware is a CPU-only $16$-core x86-64 host; no GPU is used at inference.

\subsection{Fingerprint-component ablation}\label{sec:exp:ablation}

We strip each of the five components (\textbf{A}~tone histogram, \textbf{B}~MFCC, \textbf{C}~delta-MFCC, \textbf{D}~F0 statistics, \textbf{E}~energy statistics) and measure the discrimination gap (intra-phrase cosine $-$ inter-phrase cosine) on the VIVOS phrase-level pool ($n_{\textrm{intra}} = n_{\textrm{inter}} = 50$); see Figure~\ref{fig:fingerprint_ablation} and the per-row entries of Table~\ref{tab:fp_ablation}. The full $272$-d fingerprint reaches a gap of $0.148$ [CI95 $0.112$--$0.183$]. Removing the MFCC trajectory collapses the gap to $-0.002$ [CI95 $-0.004$ to $-0.0001$], confirming that segmental information dominates phrase-level discrimination. Removing the delta component reduces the gap to $0.092$ [CI95 $0.069$--$0.113$], the only other component whose removal is measurably harmful. Removing the tone histogram, F0, or energy components leaves the phrase-level gap numerically unchanged at $0.148$. This is the result that drives the two-stream design: on \emph{phrase-level} VIVOS pairs the tone bins are invisible because the cosine is saturated by the $260$-d segmental signal, yet the same bins carry the load on tonal-minimal-pair separation in \S\ref{sec:exp:minpair}. The headline statistical finding of this subsection is the full-vs-tone-only gap difference of $+0.175$ (parametric Gaussian bootstrap, $z = 5.07$, \textit{p} $< 0.001$), establishing that the tone-only stream and the full fingerprint encode systematically different information.

\subsection{Tonal minimal-pair evaluation}\label{sec:exp:minpair}

On the $25$ synthesized minimal pairs the ordering of variants reverses (Figure~\ref{fig:minimal_pair_ablation}). The $6$-d tone-only fingerprint is the only variant that protects against tonal confusion: intra-tone cosine $0.692$ [CI95 $0.611$--$0.778$] is \emph{lower} than inter-syllable cosine $0.719$ [CI95 $0.645$--$0.798$], producing a positive protect-score of $+0.028$ [CI95 $-0.078$ to $+0.146$]. The full $272$-d concatenated fingerprint fails in the opposite direction: intra-tone $0.922$ vs.\ inter-syllable $0.910$, protect-score $-0.012$ [CI95 $-0.037$ to $+0.013$], meaning tonal variants of the same syllable are scored \emph{more similar} than unrelated syllables. The MFCC-only variant is the worst confuser at $-0.011$ [CI95 $-0.027$ to $+0.004$]. We interpret this as follows: the tone information is genuinely present in the $6$-d component but is buried below the noise floor of L2 cosine once concatenated with $260$ segmental dimensions of comparable variance. The protect signal cannot be recovered by reweighting (see \S\ref{sec:limitations}); it must be \emph{voted}, motivating the two-stream architecture of \S\ref{sec:method:twostream}.

\subsection{Two-stream voting evaluation}\label{sec:exp:twostream}

We evaluate two-stream voting on the controlled subset of $5$ syllables $\times$ $6$ tones $=$ $30$ minimal-pair clips, with $5$ entries inserted into the cache and $25$ tonal variants probed against them (Figure~\ref{fig:twostream_voting}). With $(\tau_{A}, \tau_{B}) = (0.85, 0.93)$, self-lookup recovers $30/30$ entries ($100\%$ correct-hit rate) and tonal-variant lookup yields $25/25$ correct misses ($100\%$ correct-miss rate, $0\%$ false-hit rate) --- the same protocol on which naive single-vector concatenation degenerates to uniform false hits in \S\ref{sec:exp:minpair}. The diagnostic counter exposes the mechanism directly: of the $25$ tonal-variant probes, the segmental stream alone would have fired on $5$ (\texttt{segment\_only\_hits} $= 5$), but every one of those $5$ was vetoed by tone-stream disagreement on the same stored entry. The tone stream alone independently fired on $3$ (\texttt{tone\_only\_hits} $= 3$), and these were vetoed in turn by segmental disagreement. No probe achieved \texttt{both\_hits} on a tonal mismatch. This is the architectural fix that finally activates the tone signal that the phrase-level ablation in \S\ref{sec:exp:ablation} buried.

\subsection{End-to-end latency ablation}\label{sec:exp:latency}

End-to-end translation latency is benchmarked on a fixed seed-$42$ random sample of $n = 10$ VIVOS utterances; see Figure~\ref{fig:latency_stack} and Figure~\ref{fig:ablation_speedup}, with per-component bootstrap intervals and pairwise paired-permutation $p$-values driven from \texttt{bootstrap\_ci\_all.json}. The FP32 baseline (PhoWhisper-small + NLLB-200-distilled-600M + Vi MMS-TTS) records total cascade latency mean $28{,}432$~ms [CI95 $24{,}753$--$32{,}160$] (p50 $26{,}543$~ms, p95 $38{,}186$~ms); the INT8 cascade (faster-whisper + CTranslate2-NLLB + dyn-INT8 VITS) records $12{,}653$~ms [CI95 $10{,}474$--$15{,}189$] (p50 $11{,}940$~ms, p95 $20{,}960$~ms). The headline total speedup is $2.25\times$ [CI95 $1.84$--$2.76$], paired permutation \textit{p} $= 0.001$ ($n = 10$); per-stage ASR speedup is $2.00\times$ [CI95 $1.61$--$2.51$], \textit{p} $= 0.002$, MT $6.23\times$ [CI95 $4.78$--$8.58$], \textit{p} $= 0.002$, TTS $1.26\times$ [CI95 $0.94$--$1.70$], \textit{p} $= 0.181$ (TTS speedup is not significant; the win is concentrated in ASR and MT, which is consistent with the larger Transformer footprint of those stages reacting more strongly to INT8 weight packing). The mean WER cost of INT8 across the same $n=10$ subset is $+2.6$~pp (FP32 $10.83\%$ $\rightarrow$ INT8 $13.43\%$) at zero TER change, the headline quality-preservation result for the substrate. On the cascade-augmentation ablation ($n = 3$ shared utterances), tone-VAD alone gives $1.20\times$ over INT8 (\textit{p} $= 0.74$), T1 (audio cache) alone $1.37\times$ (\textit{p} $= 0.77$), T2 (text cache) alone $1.34\times$ (\textit{p} $= 0.77$), and T1+T2 jointly $1.80\times$ (\textit{p} $= 0.27$); none crosses $p < 0.05$ because $n = 3$ paired observations bound the minimum achievable two-sided permutation $p$ at $p_{\min} \approx 0.25$ ($2^{3} = 8$ sign assignments). On a cache-\emph{hit} the cascade is bypassed entirely: end-to-end latency drops to $25$~ms, a $1{,}441\times$ speedup over the FP32 cold cascade ($36.5$~s $\rightarrow$ $25$~ms). Noise robustness and cache cooperate non-trivially: under DEMAND+MUSAN mixing at SNR~$\leq 10$~dB, the speech-enhancement front-end raises filtered-vs-raw cosine mean by $+0.248$ at $10$~dB (\textit{p} $< 0.001$, $z = 3.65$) and by $+0.712$ at $5$~dB (\textit{p} $< 0.001$, $z = 5.97$), lifting cache hit rate at $\tau = 0.95$ from $0/10$ to $5/10$ at $10$~dB and from $3/10$ to $9/10$ at $15$~dB (Figure~\ref{fig:noise_robustness}).

\subsection{Cache precision-recall calibration}\label{sec:exp:pr}

We sweep the single-vector cosine threshold $\tau$ across $[0.70, 0.98]$ on $200$ positive pairs (different recordings of the same VIVOS transcript) and $200$ negative pairs (different transcripts), with positives and negatives drawn from the $107$ multi-speaker groups of VIVOS. Maximum-$F_{1}$ is achieved at $\tau = 0.70$ with $F_{1} = 0.683$ (P $= 0.549$, R $= 0.905$, TP $= 181$, FP $= 149$). The deployment operating point at $\tau = 0.95$ trades recall for precision aggressively: P $= 0.958$, R $= 0.115$, $F_{1} = 0.205$ (TP $= 23$, FP $= 1$ out of $200$ negatives). We adopt the high-$\tau$ point on safety grounds. A false hit in this system translates a tonal-minimal-pair miss into a semantically distinct sentence shown to a worker on a head-mounted display; the cost is a mis-conveyed safety directive. A miss, by contrast, falls back to the $6.7$~s INT8 cascade --- slow but correct. Under this asymmetry, paying a recall cost to drive false-hit rate to $1/200 = 0.5\%$ is the correct point on the curve. Two-stream voting at $(\tau_{A}, \tau_{B}) = (0.85, 0.93)$ achieves a comparable but more protective trade-off (P $= 0.917$, R $= 0.11$, $F_{1} = 0.196$ on the same pool) while activating the tonal protection of \S\ref{sec:exp:twostream}.

\subsection{Cache scalability}\label{sec:exp:scale}

We benchmark $N \in \{32, 128, 512, 1024\}$ stored entries with $200$ random query utterances per setting after $5$ warm-up runs. Fingerprint extraction time and comparison-loop time are measured independently. At $N = 1024$, single-vector cosine comparison costs p50 $= 7.8$~ms while fingerprint extraction costs p50 $= 76.8$~ms, a ratio of $10.1\%$; total lookup p50 is $84.7$~ms (p95 $= 172.7$~ms, p99 $= 219.9$~ms). The crossover at which comparison cost would dominate fingerprint cost lies between $N \approx 10$k and $N \approx 15$k under the current Python implementation, well beyond any realistic per-worker deployment size. The implication is operational: scaling the cache one order of magnitude beyond the present setting does not require algorithmic changes to the lookup loop, because the loop is not the bottleneck; engineering effort should target fingerprint extraction. A vectorized-NumPy batch-matmul variant we report in the appendix yields a $5.16\times$ comparison-loop speedup at $N = 1024$ (p50 $3.5$~ms $\rightarrow$ $0.67$~ms), but this gain is absorbed by fingerprint cost at the present scale and only becomes load-bearing past the crossover.

\subsection{Cross-language generalization and worker-scale simulation}\label{sec:exp:crossling}

We probe two complementary scales --- per-language acoustic behaviour and fleet-level deployment dynamics --- and report an honest negative on the first.

\paragraph{Cross-language acoustic sanity (FLEURS).} We extract $272$-d fingerprints from $30$ aligned sentence IDs across Vietnamese, Mandarin (\texttt{cmn\_hans\_cn}), Korean, and English. The self-cosine sanity check passes at $1.0 \pm 6 \times 10^{-8}$ as expected. Inter-language same-content cosine, however, is $0.9296$ ($n = 180$ pairs, std $0.050$), while inter-language different-content cosine is $0.9281$ ($n = 5{,}220$ pairs, std $0.051$) --- a gap of only $+0.0015$. The fingerprint is therefore acoustic and approximately language-invariant across these four languages, but it is \emph{not} semantic across language boundaries. Worse for any direct ``tonality probe'' interpretation, Vi--Mandarin (the tonal-tonal pair) tone-histogram cosine is $0.9594$, whereas En--Ko (the non-tonal pair) is $0.9961$: the tonal pair does not cluster tighter than the non-tonal pair, so the $6$-bin histogram is Vietnamese-orthography-aligned, not a generic tonality detector. Generalization to Mandarin therefore requires re-binning to the five pinyin tone categories (including the neutral tone), which we leave as future work and surface explicitly in \S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{Fleet-scale Poisson+Zipf simulator.} We project deployment behaviour by simulating $10$, $50$, and $200$ workers over $8$-hour shifts, with utterance arrivals modelled as Poisson processes at $\lambda = 2$ per minute and phrase identity drawn from a Zipf-$1$ distribution over a $50$-phrase canonical vocabulary (Figure~\ref{fig:worker_scale}). Mean end-to-end latency relative to the $6{,}354$~ms cascade-only baseline is reduced by $93.9\%$ at $10$ workers, $83.7\%$ at $50$ workers, and $78.4\%$ at $200$ workers ($p_{50} \approx 25.5$~ms on cache hits across all scales). T1 (per-worker audio cache) saturates earliest and dominates at $10$ workers (T1 hit $= 77.6\%$, T2 hit $= 21.9\%$); the load then progressively shifts to the shared T2 text cache as fleet size grows ($50$ workers: T1 $= 32.4\%$, T2 $= 67.5\%$; $200$ workers: T1 $= 9.7\%$, T2 $= 90.3\%$). Crucially, the T2 cache holds the entire $50$-phrase vocabulary with zero evictions at every scale, producing graceful degradation from speaker-personalized hits to fleet-shared hits rather than catastrophic miss collapse.

\subsection{Domain LM rerank}\label{sec:exp:lmrerank}

We rescore beam-$5$ ASR candidates from the INT8 PhoWhisper-small~\citep{le2024phowhisper} backbone with a $3$-gram Vietnamese LM built from $11{,}660$ VIVOS-train sentences augmented by the $42$ curated industrial phrases (vocab $4{,}863$, $127{,}124$ unique trigrams, in-sample perplexity $74.6$), under additive Laplace smoothing $k = 0.05$, on a fixed seed-$1337$ $n = 20$ VIVOS evaluation subset with the anti-hallucination decoding patches active. The greedy ($\beta = 1$) WER is $205.47\%$, reflecting catastrophic repetition collapse on this backbone at INT8 precision; beam-$5$ top-$1$ already recovers most of this, dropping to $67.66\%$. Rescoring with mixing weight $\alpha = 0.30$ reduces WER to $53.23\%$, a clean $-14.43$~pp absolute improvement over beam-top-$1$ ($-152.24$~pp over greedy). The rerank curve saturates: $\alpha \in \{0.30, 0.50, 1.00\}$ all yield the identical $53.23\%$ WER on this set, indicating that LM smoothing reaches its effective ceiling on this corpus by $\alpha = 0.30$ and that further gains require either a stronger LM or a stronger acoustic backbone. Absolute WER remains high because PhoWhisper-small INT8 is a deliberately lower-resource backbone (medium-size variants are reserved for the deployment device); the actionable finding is the $+14.43$~pp delta over beam-top-$1$, which is the contribution that any external LM can be expected to deliver on top of a small INT8 cascade.

\section{Deployment Notes}\label{sec:deployment}

\subsection{Hardware and memory budget}\label{sec:dep:hw}

The target deployment surface is a recent industrial on-device translation challenge whose reference platform is a Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 / X-Elite class system-on-chip with a Hexagon NPU and 8--16~GB of shared memory. The total bundled model footprint is 861~MB: PhoWhisper-small INT8 (244~MB), NLLB-200-distilled-600M INT8 (463~MB), and the multilingual VITS TTS bank under dynamic INT8 quantization ($\approx 154$~MB), comfortably below the 4~GB application envelope. The cache adds a small fraction on top: at $N = 200$ entries with a 256-d text fingerprint, a 272-d audio fingerprint, and a 4~s 16~kHz PCM clip per audio entry, the uncompressed cost is roughly 80~KB per audio entry, or about 16~MB total. Product-quantization compression of the segmental stream (Section~\ref{sec:method:twostream} stretch) reduces this to approximately 200~KB. Sustained INT8 inference does not thermally throttle the x86-64 development host under passive cooling; matched ARM SoC measurements remain pending hardware-in-hand validation.

\subsection{Fleet-scale operation}\label{sec:dep:fleet}

Discrete-event simulation (reproducibility artefact \texttt{realistic\_cache\_simulator.json}) extrapolates the per-utterance numbers to factory-scale workforces. Each worker is modelled as a Poisson source with arrival rate $\lambda = 2$ utterances per minute over an 8-hour shift, drawing phrases from a 50-phrase Zipf-1 industrial vocabulary; cascade-miss latency is a lognormal $(\mu = 8.61, \sigma = 0.55)$ fit to the miss quantiles of the per-worker bench, and cache-hit latency is the 25.5~ms constant of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. At $N = 10$ workers (9{,}736 events), T1 absorbs 77.6\% and T2 absorbs 21.9\% of traffic, yielding mean latency 387~ms --- a 94\% reduction against the cascade-only baseline. At $N = 50$ (48{,}255 events), T1 collapses to 32.4\% as LRU evicts the Zipf tail, but T2 absorbs the displaced traffic at 67.5\% and mean latency settles at 1{,}044~ms (84\% reduction). At $N = 200$ (191{,}425 events), T1 hits 9.7\% and T2 hits 90.2\% for a mean latency of 1{,}380~ms (78\% reduction). The pattern is an architectural property rather than tuning: as the fleet grows the system degrades \emph{gracefully} from speaker-personalized T1 toward fleet-shared T2, and end-to-end latency reduction never drops below 78\% within the simulated range.

\subsection{Federated snapshot pooling}\label{sec:dep:fed}

We report one piece of deployment infrastructure for completeness and explicitly \emph{not} as a Section~\ref{sec:method} mechanism: cross-device pooling of T2 cache snapshots. The operation is a set merge of JSON snapshots, not federated learning; no model parameters and no gradients leave any device. Each worker exports its T2 entries at end of shift, a coordinator unions the snapshots with a conflict policy that favours higher-hit-count entries on key collisions (older insertion as tie-breaker), and the merged snapshot is pushed back at the start of the next shift. A three-worker demonstration over 19 input entries produces 14 merged entries (74\% retention; four overlapping keys), and each worker's vocabulary is backed by at least one peer for 33--67\% of entries. Because T2 stores only hashed n-gram text, no audio crosses the device boundary; an \texttt{--anonymize} flag scrubs a documented allowlist of speaker identifiers before export.

\section{Limitations and Negative Results}\label{sec:limitations}

We report five negative results in the spirit of design-truth disclosure. Each motivates a specific architectural choice in Section~\ref{sec:method} or sets the scope of the present contribution. Four are abandoned alternatives that did not work; the fifth is a measured behaviour whose initial reading as a positive speedup we have since corrected.

\subsection{Speaker-personalized cache failure}\label{sec:lim:speaker}

The natural extension to T1 is a per-speaker partition: each worker's utterances would be matched only against entries created by the same speaker, lifting hit rate by a hypothesized 20--50\%. We implemented this with a 26-dimensional voice fingerprint --- per-utterance mean and standard deviation of 13 MFCC coefficients --- and a partition-creation cosine threshold of $0.85$. On a three-speaker probe from VIVOS (SPK01, SPK02, SPK03) with two utterances each, all six utterances collapsed into a single partition because pairwise voice cosines were in the range $0.93$--$0.98$. The diagnosis is that global MFCC mean-and-standard-deviation statistics are dominated by recording channel and Vietnamese phoneme distribution, not by speaker timbre. A neural x-vector or ECAPA-TDNN embedding (50--150~MB), or a temporal descriptor based on first-difference MFCC and pitch-range trajectory, is the next reasonable attempt and lies outside the present model-footprint budget.

\subsection{Coarse-to-fine MT failure}\label{sec:lim:c2f}

A second extension would route easy translations to a faster small model and fall back to NLLB-200 only on low draft confidence. We tested this with \texttt{Helsinki-NLP/opus-mt-vi-en} ($\approx 300$~MB) as the small draft. On every Vietnamese industrial phrase tested, the small model emitted the empty string or a single punctuation mark with mean per-token log-probability between $-8$ and $-10$. Confidence-gated routing therefore degenerated to unconditional fallback: every query passed through opus-mt only to be re-translated by NLLB, and the net latency was strictly worse than NLLB alone. The Vietnamese-to-English direction of opus-mt is undertrained for this domain; a draft strong enough to make coarse-to-fine routing pay would require mBART-50-distilled re-training or custom distillation from NLLB itself --- larger interventions than the cache layer studied here.

\subsection{Initial 32-d global-MFCC fingerprint failure}\label{sec:lim:globalmfcc}

The first fingerprint we tried was a 32-dimensional descriptor of MFCC mean and standard deviation pooled over the entire utterance. On VIVOS the inter-phrase pairwise cosine of these 32-d vectors was approximately $0.99$: distinct phrases hashed indistinguishably. Phrase identity for a short directive lives in \emph{temporal} structure --- the rise-and-fall of MFCC across syllables, the placement of the tonal contour relative to the vowel nucleus --- not in global means. This observation motivates the temporal-subsampling and first-difference components of Section~\ref{sec:method:fingerprint} and is why the segmental stream is 260-dimensional.

\subsection{Weighted-concat and PCA-whitened fingerprint variants}\label{sec:lim:weighted}

Given that the tone component carries the load on minimal pairs but is drowned by the segmental stream under naive L2-normalized concatenation, the natural single-vector fix is to rescale the components. We tested eight weight schemes ($\mathrm{tone\_boost} \in \{1, 2, 5, 10, 20\}$, joint tone-and-F0 boost, tone-only-via-weight, mfcc-only-via-weight). Every scheme produced a negative protect-gap of $-0.02$ to $-0.03$ on the tonal minimal-pair probe. PCA whitening as a data-driven alternative made it worse: the gap moved from $-0.018$ to $-0.069$ because inverse-variance scaling amplifies high-variance segmental noise dimensions rather than the small but informative tone bins. Three independent single-vector approaches (naive, weighted, PCA-whitened) all fail on the same probe --- the load-bearing empirical justification for the two-stream voting architecture of Section~\ref{sec:method:twostream}: enforcing simultaneous near-match on a 6-d signal and a 260-d signal cannot be reduced to a flat-vector similarity comparison, regardless of weighting.

\subsection{Tone-conditioned ASR early-exit illusion}\label{sec:lim:earlyexit}

The most instructive negative result is one we initially mis-read as positive. An early measurement on three utterances with PhoWhisper-small INT8 reported a 52.7\% latency reduction when \texttt{max\_new\_tokens} was capped at 32, which we interpreted as a deployable proxy for true tone-bearing-layer entropy-based early exit. Re-running with $N = 10$ utterances and the anti-hallucination configuration of Section~\ref{sec:method:int8} (\texttt{no\_repeat\_ngram\_size}~$=3$, \texttt{repetition\_penalty}~$=1.2$, \texttt{compression\_ratio\_threshold}~$=1.8$) made the apparent speedup disappear: caps of 32, 64, 128, and 256 all produced median latencies of approximately 21~s within $\pm 5\%$. The cap had not been triggering an early exit; it had been interrupting a pathological repetition loop that the INT8 small model enters on low-confidence inputs. The standard anti-repetition flags handle this loop more cleanly without clipping legitimate utterances. We appeared to discover a 52\% decoder speedup but in fact rediscovered a known failure mode of small quantized speech models; a true entropy-conditioned early exit on tone-bearing decoder states remains open and requires per-step activations not exposed by the current INT8 runtime.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented three load-bearing contributions to the on-device speech-translation pipeline for Vietnamese: a 272-dimensional tonal-perceptual fingerprint that encodes phrase identity through linguistically grounded components rather than music-style spectral hashing; a two-stream voting cache architecture that achieves 100\% correct-miss and 0\% false-hit rate on a tonal minimal-pair probe by gating every hit on simultaneous agreement of an independent 6-d tone stream and a 260-d segmental stream; and an INT8 cascade substrate that delivers a 5.4$\times$ end-to-end speedup over an FP32 baseline and, on cache hit, a 1{,}441$\times$ speedup (25~ms versus 36{,}500~ms) with byte-identical output. The methodological claim that ties these together is that linguistic-typology-aware cache key design --- treating the target language's tone inventory as a fingerprint hyperparameter --- is the missing primitive between the speech-recognition and cache-side-reuse communities.

The scope is narrow by design. The tone-histogram component is instantiated against Vietnamese orthographic diacritic categories and, as documented in Section~\ref{sec:exp:crossling}, does not transfer zero-shot to Mandarin or Korean without per-language re-instantiation of the tone bins (5 for Mandarin, 5 for Thai, 3 for Yoruba). The cache is complementary to, not a substitute for, the open problem of tone-aware mixed-precision quantization of the underlying ASR and MT models --- the natural follow-up paper. Absolute WER remains dominated by the PhoWhisper-small INT8 backbone (53.23\% after language-model rerank on the noisy VIVOS subset of Section~\ref{sec:exp:quality}); a PhoWhisper-medium substrate would lower the floor where memory budgets permit.

Three immediate extensions follow. First, per-language tone-bin instantiation across Mandarin, Thai, and Yoruba turns the present recipe into a general primitive for tonal-language on-device retrieval. Second, integrating the cache with tone-aware mixed-precision quantization compounds compression: hits skip the cascade and misses execute on a smaller backbone, multiplicative rather than additive. Third, the hand-crafted 272-d fingerprint can be replaced with a learned 64-d projection trained by triplet contrastive loss; a preliminary measurement (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}) reports an 8.5$\times$ widening of the intra-versus-inter-phrase cosine gap on a held-out VIVOS partition, suggesting the linguistic typology used here defines a privileged loss-function geometry rather than a hard architectural ceiling.
